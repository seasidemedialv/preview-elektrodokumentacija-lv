Residual current device (RCD) testing

Residual current device testing shows whether the RCD disconnects the circuit when current leaks outside the conductors, and how quickly.

An RCD, or residual current device, is also called a differential protection device. Since 1 July 2025 LBN 261-15 has set out precisely in which circuits it is needed, so the test looks both at whether the device is there and whether it works.

Where an RCD is required under LBN 261-15

Point 28.¹ of the building standard requires residual current protection with a rated operating current of no more than 30 mA in the following alternating current circuits:

  • sockets up to 32 A for general use, used by persons not trained in electrical engineering;
  • sockets up to 32 A intended for connecting portable equipment used outdoors;
  • electric lighting circuits in household electrical installations.

Point 28.² requires a 300–500 mA device on the main incoming supply in the main incoming distribution board of single-family residential buildings, or an alternative technical solution. Point 28.³ requires the class of the device to be chosen according to the type of equipment to be connected. The requirements of points 28.¹ and 28.² have been in force since 1 July 2025 and apply to new and altered installations; for an older installation the test records where there is no such device.

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 294 of 9 June 2015, points 28.¹–28.³ as worded by Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025), likumi.lv/ta/id/274674.

RCD testing: what is measured

The test button on the device only checks whether the mechanism trips. An instrument determines at what residual current the device operates and in how long. The test also notes which circuits have a device and which do not, compared with the list in point 28.¹.

Sub-point 53.4 of LBN 261-15 and the report in Annex 6 of Regulation No. 238 do not name residual current device testing separately. That is why it is ordered together with the other measurements, or on its own when you or the client need to know the condition of the devices.

When testing a differential protection device is needed

  • After a new installation or a distribution board replacement, when point 52 of LBN 261-15 requires testing before putting into use.
  • When a new device has been fitted in the distribution board and you need to know whether it suits the circuit.
  • When the device trips for no obvious reason and you need to understand whether the problem is in the device or the installation.
  • Before buying a flat or house, where the test can be useful.

If a device does not trip or is missing where the regulations require it, an electrician does the installation and repair, for example electrical installation work in Riga. Afterwards the test is repeated.

Residual current device (RCD) testing in a flat and private house

In a flat the test covers the flat’s distribution board: whether the socket and lighting circuits have a 30 mA device and whether it operates. A private house adds the incoming distribution board, where in a new or altered installation point 28.² requires a 300–500 mA device on the main incoming supply or an alternative solution, and outdoor sockets, for which point 28.¹ requires 30 mA protection.

If the distribution board also has a circuit for a solar panel inverter or electric car charger, the test records what type of device is fitted there, because point 28.³ requires the class to be chosen according to the connected equipment.

How the test goes

  1. Inspecting the distribution board The devices, their rated operating current and type, and the circuits they protect are noted.
  2. Comparison with the regulations It is checked whether the circuits named in point 28.¹ are protected and whether the house’s incoming supply has the device named in point 28.².
  3. Measurement The trip current and time are determined for each device.
  4. Result The results are recorded in the measurement report together with the other measurements.

What to prepare for the test

At the moment of measurement the device switches off its circuit, so save your computer work and know which appliances have to be switched on again afterwards. If the circuits are not labelled in the distribution board, note which rooms and equipment matter to you.

Questions: Residual current device (RCD) testing

Is it enough to press the test button on an RCD?

The test button shows that the mechanism trips. It does not measure the trip current and time; an instrument determines those.

Does a 300–500 mA device have to be fitted in an old house?

Point 28.² has been in force since 1 July 2025. Ask the building authority whether and when it applies to your existing house, especially if you are planning alterations.

Why does the RCD trip for no reason?

The cause may be in the device, an appliance or the installation’s insulation. A test with an insulation measurement helps to understand where to look, while the repair is done by an electrician.
