Electrical installation testing in a private house

Electrical installation testing in a private house is broader than in a flat: it adds the incoming distribution board, the house’s own earth loop, outdoor lines and often a sauna, garage or outbuilding.

If the house is being put into use, the building authority may ask for test documentation. In other cases the owner or a buyer orders the test.

What is tested in a house and how it differs from a flat

In a house, the owner is responsible for the whole installation from the incoming supply to the last socket, so the test also includes what in a flat belongs to the building as a whole.

Part What is checked Regulation
Incoming distribution board Main protective device, number of phases, a 300–500 mA protective device on the main incoming supply or an alternative solution LBN 261-15, points 8.3 and 28.²
Earthing Loop resistance and connection to the parts to be earthed LBN 261-15, sub-point 53.4
Circuits Insulation, phase-neutral loop, 30 mA devices for sockets and lighting LBN 261-15, points 28.¹, 53.4, 54
Outdoor sockets and lighting 30 mA protection for sockets for outdoor equipment; outdoor lighting equipment is a location with special requirements LBN 261-15, points 28.¹ and 27.9

Since 1 July 2025 the residual current device requirements (points 28.¹ and 28.²) have applied to new and altered installations; for an older installation the report records where there is no such device. Point 8.3 states that in a residential building a supply with a protective device larger than 32 A is connected only in three-phase form. If a capacity increase is planned for the house, mention it in the form before the test.

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Cabinet Regulation No. 529 (likumi.lv/ta/id/269164), Regulation No. 238 (likumi.lv/ta/id/281646, in force until 1 January 2027).

Electrical installation testing in a sauna and garage

Point 27 of LBN 261-15 lists locations where there are special requirements for electrical installations. On a private house plot the most common are rooms with a bath or shower, places with sauna heaters and outdoor lighting equipment. So electrical installation testing in a sauna and shower room is a separate part of the house test.

A garage and an outbuilding usually receive power through a cable from the house distribution board. The test measures this cable’s insulation, the phase-neutral loop and the protective conductor connection, and checks whether the sockets have a residual current device. If the sauna, garage or outbuilding needs documents for putting into use, see the page sauna, garage and outbuilding.

When a house test is required and when it can be useful

  • Required: for a Group 2 residential building that the owner has built personally (Construction Law, Section 19.¹, paragraph three), Cabinet Regulation No. 529, points 157.7.1 and 168.1, requires electrical installation test documentation for putting into use.
  • Required: the periodic inspection once every 10 years under the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238); the regulations are in force until 1 January 2027.
  • Recommended: before reconnection after a long break, Sadales tīkls recommends having the installation checked.
  • Can be useful: before buying a house or if an old installation comes to light.

Putting a self-built house into use has its own page, self-built house, because besides measurements a diagram and other documents under point 53 of LBN 261-15 may be needed there.

How to prepare the house for testing

  1. List Write in the form which buildings on the plot are connected: house, sauna, garage, shed, greenhouse.
  2. Documents Get ready the existing design or diagram, earlier reports and, if the house is new, the papers handed over by the electrician.
  3. Access Provide access to all distribution boards, the earthing point and the buildings. During the test the circuits will be switched off for a while.
  4. Photos Attach a photo of the incoming distribution board and the meter; it helps to estimate the scope before the site visit.

Questions about these documents

Can the sauna’s electrical installation be tested together with the house?

Yes, if the sauna is fed from the same distribution board or the incoming supply of the plot. As a place with a sauna heater, a sauna is one of the locations with special requirements named in point 27 of LBN 261-15.

Does a private house have to have a 300–500 mA protective device?

Since 1 July 2025 point 28.² of LBN 261-15 has required it in the main incoming distribution board of a single-family residential building, or provides for an alternative solution. Ask the building authority how this applies to an existing house without alterations.

Another electrician installed the wiring in the house. Can you prepare the documents?

Yes. After a site visit and measurements, the documents are prepared and signed by a certified electrical specialist. If we find shortcomings, they have to be put right before the documents.
