Phase-neutral loop impedance measurements

Phase-neutral loop impedance measurements determine the total impedance of the circuit path from the phase conductor through the load point and back along the neutral conductor.

This value determines whether a circuit breaker or fuse will operate fast enough at the moment of a fault. LBN 261-15 names this measurement among the tests attached to the building acceptance statement.

What the phase-neutral loop is and why it is measured

The phase-neutral loop is a closed current path: from the transformer along the phase conductor to the socket or appliance and back along the neutral conductor. The higher the impedance of this path, the lower the current in a short circuit, and the protective device may operate more slowly.

Point 26 of LBN 261-15 requires protective devices to operate when specified current, voltage and time values are reached. The phase-neutral loop impedance is a way to check this in a particular circuit, not just on paper.

Loop measurement and residual current device: why both are needed

The phase-neutral loop impedance shows whether the circuit breaker or fuse will disconnect a short circuit. A residual current device (RCD) works differently: it responds to current leaking outside the conductors, for example through a damaged appliance casing.

For new and altered installations, since 1 July 2025 point 28.¹ of LBN 261-15 has required residual current protection of no more than 30 mA for general sockets up to 32 A, sockets for outdoor equipment and household lighting circuits. If a circuit has an RCD, a good loop result does not replace it, and the other way round. That is why both tests are often measured together, and RCD testing is described on a separate page.

When to order loop impedance measurements

Most often loop impedance measurements are needed together with insulation and earthing: for a new or altered installation before putting into use, at the periodic inspection or after significant changes mentioned in point 52 of LBN 261-15. They are ordered separately, for example, if a circuit breaker has been replaced on a circuit or a line extended and you need to know whether the protection still suits the circuit.

Before buying a house, this measurement can be useful as part of a wider check. If a bank or the seller asks for a document, ask which one exactly.

Order of measurements and result

  1. List of circuits During the site visit the electrician writes down the circuits, their protective devices and rated current.
  2. Measurement under voltage Unlike insulation, the loop is measured with the circuit live, at the furthest point of each circuit.
  3. Comparison The measured impedance is compared with the value at which the particular protective device operates within the required time.
  4. Record The result is recorded in the report for putting into use or in the periodic inspection report.

What the customer has to prepare

Access to the distribution board and all rooms where the circuits to be measured are, including the cellar, loft and outbuildings. If the circuits are labelled or there is a diagram, it shortens the work. Write in the form who will ask for the result.

Questions about these documents

Can the loop be measured without cutting the power?

Yes, the loop impedance is measured in a live circuit. A power cut is needed for the insulation measurement, if it is done on the same occasion.

May any electrician carry out phase-neutral loop measurements?

For building acceptance, point 55 of LBN 261-15 requires the tests in sub-point 53.4 to be carried out by a certified electrical specialist. For the periodic inspection, Regulation No. 238 does not name a construction specialist’s certificate, but the report in Annex 6 comes with a copy of the electrical safety group card of the person doing the work, a copy of the instrument’s calibration certificate and the electrical installation diagram.

Is the loop measurement part of the periodic inspection?

Yes. Point 57 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238) names the total impedance of the phase-neutral loop among the results recorded in the test report.

What does it mean if the loop impedance is too high?

It means that the protective device may not operate within the required time at the moment of a fault. An electrician has to remove the cause, and then the measurement is repeated.
