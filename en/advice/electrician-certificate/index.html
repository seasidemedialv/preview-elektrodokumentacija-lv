Electrician’s certificate - what types there are and how to check one

An electrician’s certificate in Latvia is not one document: there are construction practice certificates in the BIS register, certificates issued by BLEEA and LEB for specific fields of work, and the electrical safety group card, which is not a certificate.

Which one is needed depends on the job: measurements for putting a house into use, solar panel papers or a connection. This page explains how to check a certificate and what to look at in it.

What electrician certificates exist

Type What it gives Where to check
Construction practice certificate in the design of electrical installations The right to design electrical installations (Cabinet Regulation No. 169, point 8.5) BIS register of construction specialists
Construction practice certificate in managing and supervising electrical installation construction works The right to manage the construction and carry out construction supervision (Cabinet Regulation No. 169, point 8.11) BIS register of construction specialists
Non-regulated sphere certificate “Elektroietaišu montāža” (installation of electrical equipment) or “Elektroietaišu izbūve” (construction of electrical installations) Competence in installation and construction; with a specialisation, also for generating equipment The issuer: BLEEA or LEB
Electrical safety group card A work safety qualification Not a certificate in the sense used by Sadales tīkls

Competence evaluation of construction specialists and supervision of their independent practice are governed by Cabinet Regulation No. 169 (20 March 2018). Current data on construction practice certificates are in BIS (points 25.9 and 27.8).

Checked on 25 November 2026. Sources: Cabinet Regulation No. 169 (likumi.lv/ta/id/298177), LBN 261-15 point 55 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Sadales tīkls: certification requirements for installers of generating equipment and advice on choosing an electrician (sadalestikls.lv).

When the regulations require a certificate

  • Measurements at building acceptance into use: point 55 of LBN 261-15 requires the sub-point 53.4 tests (wire and cable resistance, phase-neutral loop, earthing, bonds) to be carried out by a certified electrical specialist certified by an accredited body to LVS EN ISO/IEC 17024.
  • Microgenerator up to 11.1 kW: Sadales tīkls accepts the inverter settings protocol only from a specialist with a BLEEA or LEB certificate specialising in power stations or generating equipment, and requires such a specialist to also carry out the installation and adjust the inverter.
  • Customer installation for a new connection: Sadales tīkls writes that it may be built by a qualified electrical specialist or an electrical installation company, and recommends looking for the list of certified specialists in BIS.

For periodic testing once every 10 years, the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) do not name a building specialist certificate. However, the Annex 6 report has attached a copy of the electrical safety group card of the person doing the work, a copy of the measuring instrument’s calibration or verification certificate and an electrical installation diagram (points 1.1–1.3 of the notes to the annex).

How to check an electrician’s certificate in the BIS register of construction specialists

  1. Ask for the details The electrician’s first name, surname and certificate number. You can ask for them before ordering the work.
  2. Open BIS In the Construction Information System (bis.gov.lv), find the register of construction specialists.
  3. Search By name or certificate number. The register includes designers of electrical installations, construction works managers and construction supervisors.
  4. Compare Whether the name matches the person you are talking to, whether the certificate is valid and whether its scope fits the job.

Sadales tīkls itself, in its advice on choosing an electrician, points to the BIS register as the place to check a specialist. Check BLEEA and LEB certificates against the issuer’s data.

BLEEA certificate and LEB certificate

BLEEA is the specialised certification centre of the society “Latvijas Elektroenerģētiķu un Energobūvnieku asociācija” (the Latvian association of power engineers and energy builders), and LEB is the certification department of the society “Latvijas Elektriķu brālība” (the Latvian brotherhood of electricians). Both are accredited bodies that issue certificates in the non-regulated sphere, for example “Elektroietaišu montāža” (installation of electrical equipment) or “Elektroietaišu izbūve” (construction of electrical installations).

For solar panel and battery work, Sadales tīkls gives customers four criteria to check:

  1. the certificate was issued by an accredited body (BLEEA or LEB);
  2. the programme name shows the field of work and the generation specialisation, for example BLEEA “Elektrostaciju elektriskā daļa, t.sk. elektrodzinēji un ģeneratori (mikroģeneratori)” (the electrical part of power stations, including electric motors and generators (microgenerators));
  3. the certificate is valid;
  4. the details match an identity document.

A power station above 11.1 kW needs a regulated sphere certificate “Elektroietaišu izbūves darbu vadīšana” (managing electrical installation construction works) or “būvuzraudzība” (construction supervision) with a generation specialisation.

How to read a certificate: what to look at

When you have a copy of the certificate or the register entry in front of you, look at five things. They follow from the Sadales tīkls criteria and the classification in Cabinet Regulation No. 169:

  • the issuer: an accredited body, for example BLEEA or LEB, or a construction practice certificate in BIS;
  • the scope: design, management of construction works, construction supervision, installation or construction;
  • the specialisation in the programme name, if the job involves solar panels or a battery;
  • the validity period;
  • a first name and surname that match the person signing the document.

If any of these things does not fit the job, choose another specialist before the work starts.

The electrical safety group is not a certificate

An electrical safety group (for example, II–V) is a work safety qualification, not a building specialist certificate. Sadales tīkls states directly that electrical safety group cards are not considered a certificate for work on generating equipment. If an electrician shows only such a card as proof, it is not enough for Sadales tīkls for solar panel papers.

What to ask before ordering the work

  • Which document you need and who is asking for it: the building authority, Sadales tīkls or a buyer.
  • Whether the scope of the electrician’s certificate fits this particular document.
  • Whether the certificate is valid on the day the document will be signed.
  • Who signs the document: the same person whose certificate you checked.

The documents are prepared and signed by a certified electrical specialist. How to choose a certified electrician specifically for documents and what they may sign is explained on the page certified electrician for documents.

Questions: Electrician’s certificate

Must an electrician be in the BIS register to take measurements for putting a house into use?

Point 55 of LBN 261-15 requires a person with a certificate issued by a body accredited to LVS EN ISO/IEC 17024. Construction practice certificates are in BIS, while BLEEA and LEB certificates should be checked against the issuer’s data and the Sadales tīkls criteria.

Does a certificate have a validity period?

Yes, Sadales tīkls names the validity period as one of the criteria. Check that the certificate is valid on the day the document is signed.

Is it enough for a company to be a building contractor?

A particular person signs the document, so look at that person’s certificate, not just at the company’s status.

Can any certified electrician draw up an electrical design?

No. Design requires a construction practice certificate in the design of electrical installations (Cabinet Regulation No. 169, point 8.5).

What happens if a solar panel protocol is signed by a specialist without the required specialisation?

Sadales tīkls accepts the inverter settings protocol only from a holder of a BLEEA or LEB certificate with a generation specialisation. The same requirement applies to the installation and inverter adjustment; see the page solar panels installed by someone else.
