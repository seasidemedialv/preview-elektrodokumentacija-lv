Which documents are needed to put a house into use

Which documents are needed to put a house into use depends on what the construction started with: an explanatory note, a construction notification or a building permit.

The Building Construction Regulations (Cabinet Regulation No. 529) give a separate list for each route, and electrical documents are only one part of it. Below are all three lists, the requirements of Riga municipality and what an electrician prepares.

Which law and regulations determine it

The basis is the Construction Law, while the specific documents are determined by Cabinet Regulation No. 529 “Building Construction Regulations”. The content of the electrical part is determined by Cabinet Regulation No. 294, Latvian Construction Standard LBN 261-15 “Internal Electrical Installations of Buildings”. Cases are submitted and examined in the Construction Information System (BIS), and each municipality’s building authority works in its own territory (Cabinet Regulation No. 529, point 5).

Checked on 25 November 2026. Sources: Cabinet Regulation No. 529 Building Construction Regulations (likumi.lv/ta/id/269164), Construction Law (likumi.lv/ta/id/258572), LBN 261-15 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Riga building acceptance service (riga.lv).

If construction started with an explanatory note

An explanatory note is used to build, for example, a Group 2 single-flat house of up to 200 m², and many alterations and renovations are carried out with it. On completion, Part II of the explanatory note is submitted to the building authority with the following attached (point 157):

  • reports on the readiness of utility networks for use;
  • as-built drawings, if anything was changed during construction;
  • the construction log together with as-built records of the construction works (except in the cases named in point 120, including an owner-built house);
  • an as-built survey plan;
  • a temporary energy performance certificate;
  • if the owner built the house (Section 19.¹, paragraph three of the Construction Law): electrical installation test documentation and a test report on heating appliances and chimneys (point 157.7).

Within 10 working days the building authority checks that there is no unauthorised construction and makes a mark on completion of the works (point 160). A Group 2 single-flat house can be accepted if the general construction works and utility networks are fully completed, the façade is finished and the area on the street side is landscaped. At least one living room, a kitchen and a bathroom with connected sanitary fittings must also be fitted out (point 160.¹).

If construction started with a construction notification

A notification is used for simplified renovation and small buildings. Completion is confirmed in BIS. Reports on utility network readiness are attached (point 156.¹). For a new Group 1 residential building, auxiliary building or agricultural non-residential building, an as-built survey plan and façade photos are also attached. In these cases a construction log does not have to be kept (point 120).

If there is a building permit

On the building permit route, point 167 lists the documents. They include the construction log, the construction supervisor’s report, test protocols, acceptance records and conformity declarations for the process equipment and special systems in the construction design (if equipment safety rules require them and they are not attached to the log). There are also reports from the bodies that issued the technical conditions. For an owner-built house, point 168.1 additionally requires electrical installation test documentation.

The confirmation that the building is ready for use is approved in BIS by the construction initiator, the building contractor, the construction works manager, the construction supervisor and the author’s supervisor (point 167.¹). Once all the documents in points 167 and 168 are received, the building authority agrees an acceptance date with the construction initiator, no later than 10 working days from submission of the documents (point 171).

What is required in Riga

In Riga, buildings are accepted by the City Development Department of Riga State City Municipality. According to the municipality’s service description, in cases started after 31 December 2019 the request can be submitted in BIS. The processing time is 10 working days, and it can be extended to as long as 4 months under the Administrative Procedure Law.

Documents listed in the Riga service description:

  • Confirmation that the building is ready for use
  • As-built survey plan
  • Cadastral survey file
  • Report on the readiness of the utility network branch
  • Construction log and construction supervisor’s report
  • Acceptance records for significant structures and concealed works
  • Energy certificate (temporary)
  • Author’s supervision log, if author’s supervision took place

In the Riga region each municipality has its own building authority, and it is worth checking the local procedure, for example in Mārupe or Salaspils, on its website.

The electrical part: what an electrician prepares

Point 53 of LBN 261-15 requires a protocol with documents to be attached to the acceptance statement if the relevant works have been done. These are a corrected set of drawings, equipment data sheets, concealed works records, measurement reports on wires and cables, the phase-neutral loop and earthing, switching diagrams and an operating description. Insulation resistance is tested under point 54. The measurements are carried out by a certified electrical specialist (point 55).

We prepare this part in Riga and the Riga region; more detail: putting a house into use, the electrical part.

After acceptance: cadastre and Land Register

Registering the building in the cadastre and making an entry in the Land Register are the next steps after acceptance. Ask your building authority which documents are needed for them and whether they can be started with the same BIS submission.

After acceptance, keep copies of all documents, including the electrical part. Electrical installation test reports can be useful later, for example when selling the house or before increasing capacity. The fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) require electrical installation testing once every 10 years.

How to proceed step by step

  1. Find the process The BIS case shows whether construction started with an explanatory note, a notification or a building permit.
  2. Compare the list Mark which documents you already have and which are missing.
  3. Order what is missing The land surveyor, the energy certificate, the electrical part, the chimney inspection if it applies.
  4. Submit Attach the documents to the BIS case and follow the building authority’s response.

Questions about these documents

Does an owner-built house need more documents?

Yes, two: electrical installation test documentation and a test report on heating appliances and chimneys (Cabinet Regulation No. 529, point 157.7). Construction supervision does not have to be organised in this case.

How long does the building authority take to examine a house?

10 working days for Part II of the explanatory note (point 160); in the case of a building permit, once all documents are received the building authority sets an acceptance date no later than 10 working days from their submission (point 171).

Can the house be accepted if the finishing is not complete in all rooms?

A Group 2 single-flat house can be handed over if the general construction works, utility networks and façade finish are completed and the area on the street side is landscaped. At least one living room, a kitchen and a bathroom with connected fittings must also be fitted out (point 160.¹). The building authority decides on the particular house.

Can electrical documents be prepared after the walls have been finished?

Measurements and a diagram can be prepared. A record of covered work, however, is no longer drawn up; ask the building authority what replaces it.
