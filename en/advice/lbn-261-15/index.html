LBN 261-15 “Internal Electrical Installations of Buildings” - the main requirements

LBN 261-15 “Internal Electrical Installations of Buildings” is a Latvian construction standard approved by Cabinet Regulation No. 294 of 9 June 2015.

It determines how electrical installations are put into buildings, where residual current protection is required and which tests have to be carried out before a building is accepted into use. Here are the points that most often affect house and flat owners, with references to the text on likumi.lv.

Where to find the text of LBN 261-15 in force

The consolidated text is on likumi.lv (Cabinet Regulation No. 294). The significant 2025 amendments are Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025, in force from 1 July 2025. A newer one is Cabinet Regulation No. 385 of 7 July 2026, which deleted Chapter III¹ “Requirements for electromobility”.

The construction standard refers to standards. The list of applicable standards is published on lvs.lv; the current list is in force from 9 January 2026. Before designing or altering, check both sources, because the text changes.

Checked on 25 November 2026. LBN 261-15 text: likumi.lv/ta/id/274674. List of standards: lvs.lv/en/legislations/1351.

LBN 261-15 incoming supply requirements: above 32 A, three phases only

Point 8.3 states that in residential buildings, an electrical installation with a main protective device greater than 32 A is connected to the system operator’s low-voltage network only as a three-phase supply. If a single-phase house needs more than 32 A, the next step is three phases, not a larger single-phase fuse.

The switch is made together with Sadales tīkls. On the documents and steps: from 1 phase to 3 phases.

Protection against residual current (points 28.¹–28.³)

Since 1 July 2025 the construction standard has required residual current protection of no more than 30 mA for general sockets up to 32 A, sockets for outdoor portable equipment up to 32 A and household lighting circuits (28.¹). In the main incoming distribution board of detached residential houses, a 300–500 mA device on the common incoming supply or an alternative solution is required (28.²). The class of the device is chosen according to the type of equipment to be connected (28.³).

More on these changes and what to do in an existing house: RCD and three-phase supply requirements 2025.

Places with special requirements (point 27)

Point 27 lists places where, according to the standards, electrical installations have special requirements:

  • rooms with a bath or shower;
  • swimming pools and other pools;
  • places with sauna heating appliances;
  • construction sites;
  • agricultural and horticultural areas;
  • confined spaces with conductive surroundings;
  • caravans and caravan parks;
  • amusement parks, exhibitions, shows and stands;
  • outdoor lighting equipment;
  • ceiling and floor heating systems;
  • fire-hazardous and explosion-hazardous rooms, buildings and zones.

The requirements themselves are in the standards, not in the text of the construction standard. In practice this means that a sauna, a bathroom or underfloor heating is looked at separately during testing.

Tests before connection and putting into use (points 52–54)

Point 52: electrical installations are connected to the network after comparison with the construction design documentation and after testing of parameters. Electrical equipment is tested before putting into use and after significant changes.

Point 53 lists the documents attached to a building acceptance statement if the relevant works have been done:

  1. a set of drawings corrected as part of author’s supervision, and detailed drawings;
  2. equipment data sheets and material conformity declarations;
  3. concealed works acceptance records on the installation of electrical equipment;
  4. approved reports on wire and cable resistance, the impedance of the phase-neutral loop, the leakage resistance of earthing loops and the bond between the earth electrodes and the elements to be earthed;
  5. automatic control and switching diagrams;
  6. an operating description and safety instructions;
  7. staff training protocols.

Point 54 requires insulation resistance to be tested before acceptance. The minimum values for a newly installed installation are in Table 2 of Annex 6.

Minimum insulation resistance values (Annex 6)

Circuit Test voltage Minimum resistance
SELV and PELV 250 V more than 0.5 MΩ
Up to 500 V 500 V more than 1.0 MΩ
Above 500 V 1000 V more than 1.0 MΩ

How it is measured and what is done if the result is lower: insulation resistance measurements.

Who may carry out the measurements (point 55)

The sub-point 53.4 tests are carried out by a certified electrical specialist certified by a body accredited in Latvia that meets the requirements of standard LVS EN ISO/IEC 17024. Construction practice certificates are checked in BIS, and BLEEA and LEB certificates against the issuer’s data; how to do it: checking an electrician’s certificate.

Electric car chargers are no longer in LBN 261-15

The chapter on electromobility was deleted by Cabinet Regulation No. 385 of 7 July 2026. So when connecting a home charger, LBN 261-15 can no longer be cited as a source of requirements. What to know about connecting a charger: electric car charger check.

What to do if you have to meet these requirements

  1. Establish the situation A new building, an alteration or an existing installation without changes. This determines which points apply.
  2. Ask the building authority Which point 53 documents are needed in your case.
  3. Order measurements Order the sub-point 53.4 measurements and the point 54 insulation test from a specialist who meets point 55.
  4. Put defects right If the measurements do not comply, an electrician puts the defects right, then the measurements are repeated and the documents drawn up.

Questions about these documents

Does LBN 261-15 apply to a flat renovation?

Point 52 requires testing after significant changes to the electrical installation. Ask the building authority whether your renovation needs documents for the authority.

Do LBN 261-15 requirements have to be met in an old house where nothing is being altered?

The requirements of the construction standard are applied when installing or altering electrical installations and when accepting a building into use. Ask the building authority whether an existing installation without alteration has to be changed.

When did the RCD requirements come into force?

Points 28.¹ and 28.², as worded by Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025, have been in force since 1 July 2025.

Does LBN 261-15 set how often electrical installations have to be tested?

No. Periodic testing is set by the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238), which are in force until 1 January 2027.

Who signs the measurements for building acceptance?

Point 55: a certified electrical specialist certified by a body accredited to LVS EN ISO/IEC 17024.
