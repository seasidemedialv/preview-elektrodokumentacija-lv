As-built wiring diagram - the electrical installation as it was built

An as-built wiring diagram is a diagram of the electrical installation that shows what was actually built, not what the design intended.

Where the regulations require an electrical installation diagram

“As-built diagram” is a trade term; the regulations use other names. For acceptance of a building into use, point 53 of LBN 261-15 requires further documents to be attached if the relevant works have been done. They include a set of drawings corrected as part of author’s supervision (53.1) and the installation’s automatic control and electrical switching diagrams (53.5).

The test report in Annex 6 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238) has an electrical installation diagram attached. It shows the nominal values of the protective devices and the types and cross-sections of the outgoing cables or wires (point 1.3 of the notes to the annex). So the diagram is also useful for the periodic test once every 10 years. Regulation No. 238 is in force until 1 January 2027.

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 points 53.1 and 53.5 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Cabinet Regulation No. 238 points 56–57 and Annex 6 (likumi.lv/ta/id/281646).

Distribution board diagram and single-line diagram

A distribution board diagram shows what is in the board: the main protective device, residual current devices (RCDs), circuit breakers and which rooms or equipment each breaker supplies. A copy of this diagram fixed to the board door lets you switch off the right circuit quickly.

In a single-line diagram, all the conductors of one circuit are drawn as one line. It is compact and shows the structure of the installation from the incoming supply to the circuits: cable cross-sections, nominal values of the protective devices and circuit names.

Type of diagram What it shows Where it is useful
Distribution board diagram Devices in the board and circuit names At the board, in daily use and during repairs
Single-line diagram Incoming supply, protective devices, cables, circuits For putting into use and for test reports
Room plan with cable routes Where the cables run in the walls After alteration, before a renovation

Flat wiring diagram

In a flat the diagram usually starts at the flat’s distribution board and covers all its circuits. A flat wiring diagram is needed after alteration, can be useful when selling or renting out the flat, and helps to link each result in the test report to a specific circuit.

If the whole installation in the flat has been replaced, see also the page documents for flat rewiring.

How an as-built diagram is made on site

  1. Distribution board Each device, its nominal value and type are written down.
  2. Circuits The breakers are switched off in turn to establish which sockets, light fittings and equipment are connected to them.
  3. Routes If a room plan is needed, cable routes are traced where they can be determined without opening the walls.
  4. Comparison If there is a design or an old diagram, the differences are noted.
  5. Drawing up The diagram is drawn up and attached to the measurement documents or handed over separately.

In the form, attach a photo of the distribution board with the door open and, if there is one, the old diagram or design. While the circuits are being identified, the electricity will go off briefly in individual rooms.

It is worth keeping two copies of the finished diagram: one by the distribution board and the other with the building documents. If anything in the installation is changed later, the diagram is updated so that it matches what was built again.

Questions: As-built wiring diagram

Can an as-built diagram be drawn from photos?

No. Photos show the devices in the distribution board, but not which rooms they supply. The circuits are identified on site.

How does an as-built diagram differ from an electrical design?

A design describes what is to be built and is drawn up by a person with a certificate in the design of electrical installations. An as-built diagram shows what has already been built.

Who signs the as-built diagram?

The documents are prepared and signed by a certified electrical specialist. The diagram can be attached to the building authority case together with the measurement documents or used on its own.

Will the diagram show where a cable runs in the wall?

Only where the route can be determined without opening the wall. The diagram does not show the exact position of a cable in a wall, and this has to be borne in mind when drilling.

Can an as-built diagram be ordered without measurements?

Yes. If a test report is needed too, they are prepared together, because the results in the report are linked to the circuits in the diagram.
