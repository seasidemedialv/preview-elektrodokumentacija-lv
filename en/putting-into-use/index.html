What the building authority asks for regarding the electrical installation

Point 52 of LBN 261-15 states that electrical equipment is tested before putting into use and also after significant changes. Point 53 lists what is attached to the acceptance statement if the relevant works have been done. These are a corrected set of drawings, equipment data sheets and material conformity declarations, concealed works acceptance records, reports on wire and cable resistance, phase-neutral loop and earthing measurements, switching diagrams and an operating description.

The phase-neutral loop measurement shows whether a circuit breaker will disconnect the circuit quickly enough in case of a fault. Insulation resistance is tested under point 54 before acceptance into use. Under point 55, these tests are carried out by a certified electrical specialist certified by a body accredited to standard LVS EN ISO/IEC 17024.

The Building Construction Regulations (Cabinet Regulation No. 529) specifically mention electrical installation test documentation for a Group 2 residential house built by its owner (points 157.7.1 and 168.1). In other cases, the building authority’s requirements follow from the type of process: explanatory note, construction notification or building permit.

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 points 52–55 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Cabinet Regulation No. 529 points 157, 167 and 168 (likumi.lv/ta/id/269164).

Which page matches your situation

If you built the house yourself on your own plot of land, start with the page self-built house. There the building authority asks for electrical installation test documentation directly under a point of the regulations.

Smaller buildings have their own pages: putting a garden house into use and sauna, garage or outbuilding. For saunas and shower rooms, point 27 of LBN 261-15 sets special requirements.

If you are altering or renovating a building, see electrical installation documents after alteration. For a flat there is a separate page on documents for flat rewiring, and for an office or shop on putting commercial premises into use.

If another electrician installed the wiring and there are no papers, read electrical installation documents when someone else did the work. For an old house without documents, the page putting an old house into use will help.

For individual documents: as-built diagram, as-built documentation, concealed works records and electrical installation documents for the building authority and BIS.

Questions: Putting a house into use

Are electrical documents needed to put every house into use?

Cabinet Regulation No. 529 names them directly for a Group 2 residential house built by its owner. Point 53 of LBN 261-15 requires the protocol to be attached to the acceptance statement if the relevant works have been done. Ask the building authority what is needed in your process.

Who signs the documents for the electrical part?

The documents are prepared and signed by a certified electrical specialist. Point 55 of LBN 261-15 requires the tests in sub-point 53.4 to be carried out by a person with a certificate issued by an accredited body.

Will the building authority definitely accept the house with your documents?

The building authority makes the decision, and it assesses the whole set of documents, not just the electrical part. We prepare the electrical part according to what the regulations and the building authority require.

How long does the building authority take to examine the documents?

In the case of an explanatory note, point 160 of Cabinet Regulation No. 529 provides for the completion mark within 10 working days. In the case of a building permit, once all documents are received the building authority sets an acceptance date no later than 10 working days from their submission (point 171). These are the building authority’s deadlines.

What if the measurements reveal defects?

An electrician has to put the defects right before the documents are prepared. We do not carry out repairs, we only test and prepare the papers.
