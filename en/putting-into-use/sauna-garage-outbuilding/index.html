A sauna has a heater and damp rooms for which LBN 261-15 sets special requirements, while for a garage and a shed the outdoor sockets matter more. We carry out measurements and prepare documents for these buildings in Riga and the Riga region.

Sauna electrical installation documents and special requirements

Point 27 of LBN 261-15 names places with special requirements for electrical installations. Two of them fit a sauna building: places where sauna heating appliances are located (27.3) and rooms with a bath or shower (27.1). So the sauna documents look separately at the heater circuit, the shower room and the lighting.

In a new or altered installation, point 28.¹ requires a residual current device (RCD) with a tripping current of no more than 30 mA for household lighting circuits and general sockets up to 32 A. The class of the residual current device, in the heater circuit too, is chosen according to the type of equipment to be connected (point 28.³).

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 points 27, 28.¹–28.³, 52–55 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674).

Putting a garage and an outbuilding into use

A garage, shed or workshop often gets electricity through a cable from the house distribution board. The test measures the insulation resistance of this cable, the phase-neutral loop (whether the breaker will disconnect the circuit in case of a fault) and the protective conductor bond to the parts that must be earthed.

For sockets to which portable equipment used outdoors up to 32 A is connected, point 28.¹ requires 30 mA protection. In a garage this applies, for example, to a socket by the door where a lawnmower or a pressure washer is plugged in.

If the outbuilding has a workshop with three-phase equipment, write that in the form. The site visit then also looks at the circuits for this equipment and their protection in the house distribution board.

Sauna inside the house The heater circuit and shower room as part of the house Whether the house documents are enough

For small buildings, Cabinet Regulation No. 529 provides for a construction notification or an explanatory note, while for some works no construction design documents are required (point 7). Which applies to your sauna or garage depends on the building’s size, group and the works, and the building authority tells you. With a notification, completion is confirmed in BIS by attaching reports on utility network readiness, and for a new Group 1 auxiliary building also an as-built survey plan and façade photos.

What to prepare before the site visit

  • The power and model of the sauna heater, if known, or a photo of the heater’s data plate.
  • Information on where the cable from the house runs: underground, overhead or along a wall.
  • Access to the house distribution board and the building’s board, if there is one.
  • The building authority’s letter or the status of the BIS case, if the process has already started.

Questions: Putting a sauna into use

Does a sauna need a separate distribution board?

Whether the existing board suits the heater circuit is assessed at the site visit. Write in the form the heater’s power and where the sauna gets its electricity.

The garage is fed from the house. Is it tested separately?

The cable from the house and the garage circuits are measured. Ask the building authority whether it wants the documents together with the house case or separately.

Do the sauna documents require the heater power?

Yes, it helps to assess the cable and protection. If you do not know the power, attach a photo of the heater’s data plate.

What if there is no RCD in the garage?

If the test shows that the required protection is missing, an electrician has to install it before the documents are prepared. We do not install it, we only test.

Do you connect sauna heaters?

No. The heater and cable are installed by an electrician, for example electricians for installation and repair work. We test what has been built and prepare the documents.
