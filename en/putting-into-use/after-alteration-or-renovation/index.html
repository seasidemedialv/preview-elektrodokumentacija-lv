LBN 261-15 requires electrical equipment to be tested not only before putting into use but also after significant changes. We test altered installations and prepare documents for houses and buildings in Riga and the Riga region.

When an alteration requires new electrical documents

Point 52 of LBN 261-15 states that electrical equipment is tested after significant changes have been made, to make sure the work complies with the construction standard and applicable standards. If the alteration installs a new distribution board, adds circuits in an extension or replaces the incoming supply, testing is carried out before the building is used.

Under the Building Construction Regulations (Cabinet Regulation No. 529), many cases of alteration and renovation proceed with an explanatory note. For Part II of it, point 157 requires as-built drawings to be attached if anything was changed during the work. The building authority determines whether your work is alteration or renovation and which process has to be started. Write in the form which process has already been started, so that at the site visit we know which document to prepare.

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 points 8.3, 28.¹, 28.², 52–54 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Cabinet Regulation No. 529 point 157 (likumi.lv/ta/id/269164).

Requirements that changed on 1 July 2025

Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025 added requirements to LBN 261-15 that have to be taken into account in an alteration. If you are altering the incoming supply or the distribution board, they apply directly to you.

Point Requirement
8.3 In a residential building, an incoming supply with a protective device greater than 32 A is connected only as a three-phase supply
28.¹ Residual current device (RCD) of no more than 30 mA for sockets up to 32 A, outdoor sockets and household lighting circuits
28.² In the main incoming distribution board of a detached residential house, a 300–500 mA device on the common incoming supply or an alternative protection solution

Ask the building authority how these requirements apply to the part of the installation that the alteration leaves untouched. In the documents we describe the actual condition.

What changes in the documents after an alteration

  1. Old diagram We compare it with what was built. If there is no diagram or it does not match, a new as-built diagram is needed.
  2. Measurements Insulation resistance under point 54, phase-neutral loop and earthing under sub-point 53.4 for the new and changed circuits.
  3. Concealed works Once works are already covered, a record of them is no longer drawn up; the building authority says which document can replace it. If the cables are still visible, request a site visit before the finishing.
  4. Documents The documents are prepared and signed by a certified electrical specialist, and you attach them to the building authority case.

What to prepare after renovation work

  • A list of what was changed in the alteration: distribution board, circuits, incoming supply, rooms.
  • The previous diagram or design and the alteration design, if there is one.
  • Who did the electrical work and whether they handed over any papers.
  • A photo of the distribution board after the alteration.

If the alteration is still under way, request a site visit while the cables are still visible. It is then easier to trace the new routes and mark them on the as-built diagram.

If another electrician did the alteration and there are no papers, the page documents when someone else built the installation will also help.

Questions about these documents

Does the whole installation have to be tested after a partial alteration?

Point 52 of LBN 261-15 speaks of testing after significant changes. The changed circuits have to be measured; ask the building authority whether it requires documents for the whole building.

Does an alteration require switching to three phases?

If the incoming supply of a residential building is planned to be greater than 32 A, point 8.3 of LBN 261-15 requires a three-phase connection. This point applies specifically to an incoming supply greater than 32 A.

Can the old test reports be used?

They describe the installation before the alteration. The changed parts need new measurements, because point 52 requires testing after changes.

Do you carry out the alteration itself?

No. The electrical work of the alteration is done by an electrician or a building contractor. We test the finished work and prepare the diagram and documents, and defects have to be put right before that.
