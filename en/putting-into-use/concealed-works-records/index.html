Concealed works records for electrical installations - before the walls are closed

Concealed works records for electrical installations have to be drawn up while the cables, junction boxes and earth electrodes are still visible, because after plastering, pouring the floor or backfilling the trench they can no longer be examined.

LBN 261-15 names them among the documents attached when a building is accepted into use. In Riga and the Riga region we visit sites where the work is not yet covered and tell you which documents can be prepared.

What concealed works are in an electrical installation

Concealed works are those that later work will hide from view. In an electrical installation these are, for example, cables in wall chases and under plaster, cables in the floor or ceiling, a cable in a trench to a garage or sauna and the earthing loop in the ground.

Point 53.3 of LBN 261-15 requires concealed works acceptance records on the installation of electrical equipment to be attached to the building acceptance statement, if such works have been done. In Riga, the document list of the city’s acceptance service mentions acceptance records for significant structures and concealed works.

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 point 53.3 (likumi.lv/ta/id/274674), Riga municipality building acceptance service (riga.lv).

When the record has to be drawn up

Work When the record is drawn up What covers it
Cables in wall chases Before plastering or filling Plaster, tiles
Cables in the floor Before the floor is poured Concrete layer
Cables in frame walls and ceilings Before the plasterboard is fitted Cladding
Underground cable to another building Before the trench is backfilled Soil
Earthing loop Before backfilling Soil, foundations

Who signs the record on your site depends on whether there is a construction works manager and a construction supervisor. Find this out from the building authority or the construction works manager before the work is covered.

What is worth writing in a concealed works record

If the construction works manager or the building authority has its own form, use it. In any case it matters that one can later understand from it exactly what was covered. For the record to be useful both to the building authority and to the next electrician, it is worth stating in it:

  • the room and the part of the wall or floor where the work was covered;
  • the cable type and cross-section, the locations of boxes and joints;
  • which distribution board circuit the cable comes from;
  • the date the work was examined and who examined it;
  • annexes: a diagram or sketch and photos with dimensions.

For an earthing loop, the number and layout of the earth electrodes is also useful in the record, because after backfilling this can only be established by measurements.

If the walls are already closed

A concealed works record is not recreated after the work has been covered; the building authority has to be asked what it accepts instead. We do not sign records for work that cannot be examined, and we do not prepare documents with an earlier date.

What can still be done after covering are measurements: insulation resistance, phase-neutral loop, earthing and bond resistance (points 53.4 and 54 of LBN 261-15), as well as a diagram of the electrical installation as built. Whether the building authority accepts them in your case instead of concealed works records has to be asked at the building authority.

How to prepare while the work is still visible

  1. Plan the time Request a site visit before the plasterers or floor layers start work, not on the same day.
  2. Take photos It is worth photographing the routes on each wall yourself with a tape measure, so that you later know where the cables run.
  3. Gather the papers Conformity declarations for cables and equipment and the design, if there is one.
  4. Write it in the form Which work will be covered, when the finishing is planned and who is asking for the records.

Questions about these documents

Can a concealed works record be drawn up from photos?

The record confirms that the work was examined before it was covered. Whether the building authority accepts photos as evidence has to be asked at the building authority; we do not sign a record on photos alone.

Does a flat renovation need concealed works records?

Point 53.3 of LBN 261-15 links them to the acceptance of a building into use. If the building authority asks for documents for a flat alteration, ask whether concealed works records are among them.

Can a wall be opened to draw up a record?

What is in the opened place can be examined. Whether this is enough for the building authority is for the authority to determine, so ask it before opening walls.

Does an earthing loop need a separate record?

An earthing loop in the ground is concealed work, so it is recorded before backfilling. Its resistance is measured separately under point 53.4 of LBN 261-15, and the result can also be checked after backfilling.
