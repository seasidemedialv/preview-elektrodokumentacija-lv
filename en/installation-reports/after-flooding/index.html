After flooding in a basement or on the ground floor, or after a water leak from above, measurements and an electrical installation report help to decide which circuits can be switched on and which have to be replaced.

Why insulation is measured after flooding

Water that has got into sockets, junction boxes, cable ducts or the distribution board lowers the insulation resistance between conductors and to earth. It cannot be seen, only measured. A measurement with a raised test voltage shows whether a circuit is dry and usable or whether there is still moisture or damaged insulation in it.

Point 54 of LBN 261-15 requires electrical installations to be tested with the same measurement before putting into use, and the minimum values are set in Table 2 of Annex 6 of the building standard.

Insulation resistance: what the result is compared with

Nominal circuit voltage Test voltage Minimum insulation resistance
SELV and PELV circuits 250 V more than 0.5 MΩ
Up to 500 V (ordinary house circuits) 500 V more than 1.0 MΩ
Above 500 V 1000 V more than 1.0 MΩ

LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 294) point 54 and Table 2 of Annex 6, likumi.lv. Checked on 25 November 2026.

If the result is below the minimum value, the circuit is left switched off, allowed to dry and measured again, or the damaged part is replaced. More on this measurement: insulation resistance measurements.

If the basement, distribution board or outdoor circuits were flooded

In a private house the lowest places are usually affected most: the basement, plant room, pump and outdoor socket circuits. If the water reached the distribution board, the protective devices and residual current device (RCD) are also inspected. In a new or altered installation, point 28.¹ of LBN 261-15 provides for residual current protection of no more than 30 mA also for sockets up to 32 A intended for connecting portable equipment used outdoors.

LBN 261-15 point 28.¹ (as worded by Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025), likumi.lv. Checked on 25 November 2026.

Order of steps after flooding

  1. Switch off the affected circuits If water reached the distribution board or sockets, switch off the affected circuits or the incoming supply, if this can be done safely.
  2. Write it down and take photos How high the water was, which rooms and circuits were affected.
  3. Site visit and measurements The electrician inspects the distribution board and measures the insulation resistance of the affected circuits.
  4. Report You receive a report: which circuits can be switched on, which must stay off and what has to be replaced.
  5. Repairs and repeat measurements An electrician replaces the damaged part, and the circuit is then measured again.

What to write in the form

  • The address and type of property.
  • When the flooding was and whether the water has gone.
  • Which rooms the water reached and how high.
  • Whether the distribution board was under water and which circuits are currently switched off.
  • Whether the report is requested by the building manager or another party.

If a third party asks for the report, attach its questions to the form.

Questions about these documents

Can the electricity be switched on after flooding if everything looks dry?

A box or duct that is dry outside can be damp inside. It is safer to measure the insulation resistance of the affected circuit first and only then switch it on.

How long should one wait before measuring?

That depends on how quickly the rooms and the electrical installation dry out. If the first measurement is below the minimum value, it is repeated later.

Is a water leak from the neighbours above also flooding?

For the electrical installation, yes: if water ran along the ceiling, light fittings or wall ducts, the affected circuits are tested in the same way.

Does the whole electrical installation have to be replaced after flooding?

Not necessarily. The measurements show which circuits meet the minimum values and which do not. The parts replaced are those that still do not meet them after drying and a repeat measurement, or that are visibly damaged.

Does the earthing have to be checked after flooding?

If water reached the earth electrode connection, the distribution board or the main earthing terminal, it is worth measuring the earthing resistance. For this, see earthing resistance measurements.
