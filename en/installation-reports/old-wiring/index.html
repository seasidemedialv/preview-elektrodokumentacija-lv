Old electrical installation site visit - what to keep and what to replace

An old electrical installation site visit is the first step if a house or flat is inherited, has been unoccupied for a long time or the electrical installation is decades old.

The report shows the technical condition: what conductors and protective devices there are, whether there is earthing and which circuits should be replaced first. That way you plan repairs on facts rather than guesses.

What the site visit shows in an old electrical installation

What is found What it means for the owner
Fuses or old circuit breakers When planning new circuits or a larger load, you have to expect the distribution board to be replaced
No residual current device (RCD) When altering, the current LBN 261-15 requirements have to be followed, see below
Two-wire installation without an earth conductor The sockets have no protective earthing
Aluminium conductors The report states which circuits they are in, and measurements show the condition of the insulation
Extension leads and circuits added later The diagram, if there is one, no longer matches what is there

If figures are needed, measurements are added to the site visit: insulation resistance, phase-neutral loop and earthing. They show whether the circuits can be used until repairs.

Aluminium wiring

Aluminium conductors are found in older buildings. The report states which circuits they are in and what condition the connections in the distribution board and visible boxes are in. If the condition of the connections raises questions, the board and boxes can be checked with a thermal camera, see thermal camera inspection of a distribution cabinet. Whether to replace the aluminium circuits at once or gradually is the owner’s decision after the report.

If you rewire: requirements since 1 July 2025

  • Residual current protection of no more than 30 mA for general sockets up to 32 A, outdoor sockets up to 32 A and lighting circuits in households (LBN 261-15 point 28.¹).
  • In the main incoming distribution board of a single-family house, residual current protection of 300–500 mA on the main incoming supply, or an alternative technical solution (point 28.²).
  • In residential buildings, a main protective device above 32 A is connected only in a three-phase arrangement (point 8.3).

LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 294) points 8.3, 28.¹ and 28.², as worded by Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025, likumi.lv. Checked on 25 November 2026.

The alteration is done by an electrician, for example, electrical installation replacement and repairs in Riga. After the alteration we prepare the measurements and documents: see electrical installation documents after alteration.

An inherited or long-unoccupied house

If the electricity supply was interrupted for a long time, Sadales tīkls recommends choosing a certified electrician and checking the internal electrical installation before reconnection; if it is not fit for use, the connection cannot be restored. No fee is paid for reconnection only if it is requested within 30 days after the previous user’s contract ended. Later you pay for the connection (€34.76 in the 0.4 kV network according to the Sadales tīkls price list, checked on 25 November 2026) and the capacity maintenance fee for up to 24 months. More: restoring an electricity connection.

Sadales tīkls, sadalestikls.lv/lv/piesleguma-atjaunosana and /lv/cenradis. Checked on 25 November 2026.

What to prepare

  • The address, type of building and approximate age of the electrical installation, if you know it.
  • Old diagrams, a design or earlier test reports, if they have survived.
  • Photos of the distribution board and meter.
  • Whether the property currently has electricity.
  • What you are planning: to live as it is, renovate in stages or replace everything.

Questions: Old electrical installation site visit

Does an old electrical installation have to be replaced?

The fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) require the electrical installation to be kept in working order and tested once every 10 years. Whether to replace it is the owner’s decision after the site visit and measurements.

Can only the distribution board be replaced and the old wiring kept?

That depends on the condition of the wiring and the measurement results. The report shows which circuits can be kept and which should be replaced.

Is a report on an old electrical installation also suitable before a purchase?

Yes. If you are still buying the house, see testing before buying a house.

Do you also replace electrical installations?

No. We test and prepare documents; replacement is done by an electrician. After the replacement we can take measurements and prepare a test report.
