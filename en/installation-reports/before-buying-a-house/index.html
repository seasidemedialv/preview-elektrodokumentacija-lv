The result is an electrical installation report, which you can use for your decision and in talks with the seller. The same applies when buying a flat or taking over a new home from a developer.

Why a buyer should test before the contract

The buyer orders this test themselves, and the report can be useful: it shows whether, after the purchase, the distribution board will have to be replaced, earthing installed or wiring re-run. If a bank is financing the purchase, it may ask for a similar paper, see electrical installation testing for a bank.

The simplest first step is to ask the seller for the latest test report. The fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) provide for electrical installation testing once every 10 years, and a report under Annex 6 is drawn up on it. If there is no report or it is old, that is already information.

Cabinet Regulation No. 238 “Fire Safety Regulations”, points 56–57, likumi.lv. Checked on 25 November 2026.

Electrical installation documents to ask for when a house is sold

If you are the seller, it is worth finding these papers in good time. If you are the buyer, ask for them before the site visit:

  • the electrical design or electrical installation diagram;
  • the latest test report (insulation, phase-neutral loop, earthing);
  • for newer houses: the documents attached to the acceptance of the building into use (LBN 261-15 point 53);
  • information on the connection capacity and number of phases;
  • if there are solar panels on the roof: the Sadales tīkls permission for parallel operation.

Taking over a new flat from a developer

Before signing the acceptance statement, it can be checked whether the circuits in the distribution board are labelled, whether there are residual current devices (RCD) and whether the measurements meet the requirements. Since 1 July 2025, point 28.¹ of LBN 261-15 has provided for residual current protection of no more than 30 mA for general sockets up to 32 A and for lighting circuits in households. Defects are easier to deal with before the statement is signed.

How the test goes

  1. Arrangement with the seller The seller has to allow access to the property and the distribution board. Measurements require the property to have electricity.
  2. Site visit The electrician looks at the distribution board, protective devices, type of conductors and earthing.
  3. Measurements, if needed If questions about insulation or earthing arise during the site visit, they can be measured.
  4. Report You receive a written report on the condition and the defects found.

What the site visit looks at in the buyer’s interest

What is inspected Why it matters to the buyer
Distribution board: fuses or circuit breakers, circuit labelling An old board means work and costs after the purchase
Residual current devices (RCD) Whether they are there and for which circuits
Earthing and sockets with an earth contact Without earthing, some appliances cannot be connected safely
Conductor material and appearance in the distribution board Aluminium or damaged conductors can mean re-wiring
Connection capacity and number of phases Whether it will be enough for the planned load

If you are planning a larger load, take point 8.3 of LBN 261-15 into account: in residential buildings, an electrical installation with a main protective device above 32 A is connected to the Sadales tīkls low-voltage network only in a three-phase arrangement. On increasing capacity, see increasing electrical capacity.

What the buyer should prepare

  • The property address and the seller’s consent to the site visit.
  • The seller’s documents from the list above, if there are any.
  • The purchase timeline: in the form, choose when the test is needed.
  • If you are interested in a particular thing, for example, whether there will be enough capacity for an electric car charger or heat pump, write that.

Questions about these documents

Can the test be done if the seller still lives in the house?

Yes, if the seller agrees and allows access to the distribution board and rooms. Agree on a time when someone from their side will be on site.

Does the report say how much repairs will cost?

The report contains the condition and the defects to be put right. The repair price is set by the electrician who will do the work.

Can a flat in an apartment building be tested?

Yes, the flat’s electrical installation from the flat’s distribution board. If the stairwell distribution board also has to be inspected, access has to be agreed with the building manager.

What happens if serious defects are found?

You will see it in the report. What to do about it is your decision: talk to the seller about the price, ask for the defects to be put right before the deal or withdraw.
