Electrical installation testing for insurance - a report on damage

Electrical installation testing for insurance is needed when, after an incident, the insurer asks for an electrician’s written assessment: what is damaged, where, and whether the electrical installation can be used.

We visit the property and prepare an electrical installation report. We do not carry out repairs, so the report is not tied to a repair order.

When an insurer asks for an electrician’s assessment

For example, after damage: a water leak from the flat above, a fire, a damaged distribution board or appliances that broke down at the same time. For insurance purposes the insurer may ask for a document describing the condition of the electrical installation in order to assess the case. Exactly which document and with what content is decided by the insurer, not the electrician, so ask the insurer first.

The test can also be ordered for an old house before a policy is taken out, to know its condition. A report can be useful then too, but whether it is required is decided by the particular insurer.

What to do before the site visit

  1. Write down what happened When and what happened, which circuits tripped, which appliances stopped working.
  2. Take photos Of the damaged places, the distribution board and the appliances, before you change or throw anything away.
  3. Keep the damaged parts If an electrician has already replaced a circuit breaker or socket, set the old one aside.
  4. Ask the insurer For the case number, what the document has to contain and the date by which it is needed.

Damage report or electrician’s report

Insurers may call the document different things: a damage report, a technical opinion, an electrician’s report. We prepare an electrical installation report. If the insurer has its own form or list of questions, mention it in the form, and after the site visit we tell you which questions we can answer from what can be seen and measured on site.

If the insurer asks about the cause of the damage, write that in the form too. The cause is harder to assess once the damaged parts have been replaced, so it is important to keep them.

What a report for an insurance case contains

Part Content
Property Address, date, what was inspected
Findings Which circuits, distribution boards and appliances are damaged, what can be seen on site
Measurements Insulation resistance or other measurements, if taken
Conclusion Whether the electrical installation can be used, what has to be replaced or repaired
Signature The documents are prepared and signed by a certified electrical specialist

After the report: repairs and repeat measurements

An electrician repairs the damage. We do not carry out repairs, but after the repair the insulation resistance and earthing can be measured and a test report prepared showing that the electrical installation meets the requirements again. Point 52 of LBN 261-15 provides for electrical equipment to be tested after significant changes too. If the damage followed a fire or flooding, see testing after a fire or testing after flooding.

LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 294) point 52, likumi.lv. Checked on 25 November 2026.

What to write in the form

  • The address and type of property: flat, private house, commercial premises.
  • What happened and when, which rooms and circuits were affected.
  • What the insurer asks for: the name of the document, questions, deadline.
  • Whether the property’s electricity is currently on and whether any circuit is switched off.
  • Whether anything has already been replaced or repaired and whether the old parts have been kept.

You can send photos of the distribution board and the damaged places after we have been in touch. They help to prepare for the site visit but do not replace it: a report is written only after an inspection on site.

Questions about these documents

Will the insurer accept this report?

The insurer decides that. So before ordering, ask what the document has to contain and write it in the form.

Do I have to wait for the insurer’s expert before contacting an electrician?

Ask your insurance company whether it wants to see the damage itself. Until then, photograph the damaged places and keep the replaced parts.

Can a report be prepared after the repair has already been done?

The current condition can be described and the installation measured after the repair. What was there before the repair can be judged only from the kept parts and photos.

Will you also repair the damage?

No, we test and prepare documents. The repair is done by an electrician, and afterwards we can prepare a measurement report.

Can only the damaged part be tested instead of the whole flat?

Yes, during the site visit only the affected part can be looked at. If the insurer wants an assessment of the whole electrical installation, write that in the form, because the scope of work and price depend on it.
