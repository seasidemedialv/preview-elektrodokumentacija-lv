Port warehouse or production building: when the ten-year interval does not fit

Jaunmīlgrāvis serves housing, the port, production, warehousing and transport services. The testing interval for a warehouse or production building is not the same for all. Point 56 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027) sets testing once every 10 years, but once every two years in an explosive environment and once a year in a chemically aggressive environment.

Environment on site How often tested (MK 238, point 56) What to state in your request
No special environment Once every 10 years The date of the last report and the number of distribution boards
Explosive environment Once every two years Which rooms or zones are of this kind
Chemically aggressive environment Once a year Which rooms are of this kind and what happens in them

Checked on 25 November 2026: Cabinet Regulation of 19 April 2016 No. 238 “Fire Safety Regulations”, sub-points 56.1 and 56.2. The regulations are in force until 1 January 2027; the text that will replace them had not yet been published on that date.

Find out from the person responsible for fire safety at your company whether your premises have either of these environments before ordering, because the date of the next report depends on it. If a warehouse has several zones, the report can cover all of them, but each will have its own interval. For how the work goes and how to prepare, see electrical installation testing for businesses.

Apartment building: shared distribution boards and a report for the manager

The typical development here is apartment buildings, historic low-rise buildings, and port and industrial sites. In an apartment building the documents fall into two parts. The shared installation, that is, the incoming supply, stairwell distribution boards, risers and basement lighting, is part of the building, and its testing is organised by the building manager. The flat’s circuits beyond the flat distribution board are the owner’s responsibility.

If you are the house representative or a board member, ask the manager for a copy of the last report. It shows which distribution boards were measured and when. Before the site visit, find out who will open the basement and the electrical room, and how many stairwells the building has. For how such a test goes, see electrical installation testing in an apartment building.

A flat owner can order measurements for their own circuits separately, regardless of the building test. The report then describes only the flat distribution board and its circuits. If the flat has an electric cooker or underfloor heating, write that, because these circuits are measured differently from lighting.

Lightning protection for buildings near the port and industry

Port and industrial sites and larger apartment buildings may have a lightning protection system. Testing it is part of the same test under the fire safety regulations, and the result is entered in the same Annex 6 report. If there is a system, do not order it separately from the other measurements: in one visit both the earthing and the connections near the ground can be measured.

In Annex 6 of MK 238 the lightning protection resistance is measured from the air terminal to the test joint. More: lightning protection testing. In your request, write whether there is a diagram of the system and where the test joints on the wall are.

Old house without documents: where to start with the electrical papers

Jaunmīlgrāvis is a historically developed part of Mīlgrāvis with port and residential functions. An old low-rise house may have neither a design nor a diagram left, and the owner finds this out only when the building authority or a buyer asks for papers. Start with what is on site now: a site visit, an as-built diagram from the traced circuits and measurements for all circuits.

In Riga buildings are accepted into use by the municipality’s City Development Department; in cases opened after 31 December 2019, the application can be submitted in BIS. The building authority determines in the particular case which documents your house needs. For the electrical part of the route for an old house, see electrical documents for an old house.

Before the site visit, gather what is available: old plans or diagrams if any have survived, the last measurement report if there was one, and information on when and who last worked on the distribution board. If some of this is missing, that is fine: a diagram can be drawn up anew from what is traced on site. For houses on the port side, access is determined by Mīlgrāvja iela and the port approach roads, so write which road to use.

Questions: Electrical papers in Jaunmīlgrāvis

Does the ten-year interval suit a port warehouse?

Only if the premises have no explosive or chemically aggressive environment. The fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027) set testing once every two years or once a year for such environments. Find out from the person responsible for fire safety at your company what environment your premises have.

Which reports on the building’s shared installation can you ask the manager for?

The main one is the Annex 6 test report of the fire safety regulations on insulation, the loop, earthing and lightning protection and, if done, the Annex 7 thermal camera report. Ask for a copy of the last report: the next test is counted from its date.

How do you start the electrical papers for an old building without documents?

With a site visit and an as-built diagram showing what has actually been built, and with measurements for all circuits. With these papers you can talk to the building authority, which determines what else your case requires. Any damage found has to be repaired before the documents are drawn up.
