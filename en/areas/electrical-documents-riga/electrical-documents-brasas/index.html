Periodic testing of a masonry building and what to keep

The typical buildings of Brasa are pre-war masonry houses, apartment buildings, hospital and institutional complexes and small commercial buildings. In an apartment building, periodic testing covers the common part: the incoming distribution board, risers, stairwell boards and earthing. If the building has lightning protection, that too.

Flats are a separate question in this test. If the building manager wants the measurements to cover flat distribution boards too, each owner has to provide access. This needs to be agreed in advance, for example with a notice in the stairwell and a list showing which flat will be accessible on which day.

Before the test, it helps if the building manager or house representative gathers earlier reports and diagrams, if there are any. They show when the last test took place, which boards were measured and whether there were non-conformities that should have been fixed since.

After the test, keep both reports with the building’s technical documentation until the next time. For what this test includes, see the page periodic electrical installation testing.

An office or clinic: equipment that cannot be switched off at any time

People live in Brasa, and there are also health care institutions, offices, educational institutions and local services here. A clinic or laboratory may have equipment that cannot simply be switched off during measurements, and an office server room runs day and night. So before the test it has to be agreed which circuits can be switched off and when.

In your request, write the floor area, the number of distribution boards, whether there is lightning protection and which equipment has to be switched off separately, for example with the institution’s technician. If you rent the premises, find out whether you or the building owner has to order the test.

A health care or educational institution may have its own internal document requirements set by its management or supervising body. If there are any, send them with your request, so that the report covers exactly the parts required and does not have to be added to later.

For how business and institution premises are tested, see the page electrical installation testing for businesses.

A thermal camera report together with periodic testing

An institutional complex or a large masonry house has many distribution boards, each with dozens of connections. Point 58 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) provides that the quality of contact joints is checked with a thermal camera together with periodic testing. A report on the results is drawn up following Annex 7. The regulations name the places to be checked as examples:

  • junction boxes;
  • electrical distribution cabinets (distribution boards);
  • places where protective devices are installed.

Checked 25 November 2026: Cabinet Regulation No. 238 of 19 April 2016, “Fire Safety Regulations”, point 58 and Annex 7. The regulations are in force until 1 January 2027; the text of their successor has not yet been published on this date.

A thermal camera shows overheated connections only if current flows through them, so the inspection is planned during working hours, when the premises carry their normal load. For this work, see the page thermal camera inspection of a distribution board.

If the thermal camera shows a raised temperature at a connection, the report records the place and the measured value. An electrician tightens or replaces the connection, and a repeat image shows that the heating no longer appears.

More capacity for a flat in a masonry house

If you plan an induction hob, electric heating or other powerful equipment in a flat in an apartment building, you may need a higher permitted capacity. It is requested from Sadales tīkls on e-st.lv. Sadales tīkls states that a flat owner must agree any changes to the building’s internal electrical networks with the managing company in writing and attach the approval to the application.

Before applying, find out whether the flat’s distribution board and the cable to the stairwell can take more capacity. This can be seen at the site visit, and then you know whether the application is enough or the board has to be rebuilt first. For the steps, see the page increasing permitted capacity.

If the house is old and the riser has not been replaced, more capacity may also depend on the building’s common part. Then the question is not only about your flat, and the managing company may ask for the condition of the building’s distribution board to be assessed before giving approval.

Questions: Electrical installation testing in Brasa

When is a thermal camera report needed with periodic testing?

The fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) provide that contact joints are checked with a thermal camera together with periodic testing, and a separate report following Annex 7 is drawn up. So at the time of periodic testing both reports go together.

Is the managing company’s consent needed to increase capacity for a flat?

Yes. Sadales tīkls states that a flat owner must agree changes to the building’s internal electrical networks with the managing company in writing, and this approval has to be attached to the service application on e-st.lv. Start with the managing company before you submit the application.

Which documents must be kept until the next test?

Keep the measurement test report (Annex 6), the thermal camera report (Annex 7) and the electrical installation diagram, if there is one. Reports drawn up after 19 April 2016 are valid until the next test. If the installation has been changed in the meantime, add the documents for that work too.
