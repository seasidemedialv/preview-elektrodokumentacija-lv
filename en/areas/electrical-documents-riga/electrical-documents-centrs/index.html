An office, shop or café on the ground floor needs a report on the distribution board and circuits of its own premises that can be shown to the building owner or tenant. For a flat on the upper floors the question is different: what happened to the circuits during renovation and which paper remains once the wires are covered up.

An office or shop report: what has to be recorded in it

In Riga centre premises are used by offices, retail, hospitality, cultural institutions and professional services, and people live in the same buildings. So one address may have several distribution boards with different users. Before ordering a test, find out which part the report will cover: only the premises you rent or the whole building.

If the building already has a report from an earlier test, attach a copy to your request. It shows which boards were measured and how the circuits were numbered. The new report can then be drawn up in the same order, and the tenant and owner can easily compare both results.

The fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) set the test report form in Annex 6. The table shows what the report has to contain and why it matters to you. For the order of work and preparation in business premises, see the page electrical installation testing for businesses.

Entry in the report (Annex 6) What it means for you
Insulation resistance Whether wire insulation between phases and to earth meets the standard on each circuit
Total impedance of the phase-neutral loop Whether the protective breaker will switch off a fault quickly enough
Earthing system resistance and earth conductor continuity Whether the protective conductor in the premises is really connected to earth
Lightning protection system To be entered if the building has one
Measuring instrument details and calibration or verification certificate number What was used to measure; without these details a report is hard to check

Checked 25 November 2026: Cabinet Regulation No. 238 of 19 April 2016, “Fire Safety Regulations”, point 57 and Annex 6. The regulations are in force until 1 January 2027; the text of their successor has not yet been published on this date.

A flat in an Art Nouveau house after rebuilding the circuits

The typical buildings of Riga centre are Art Nouveau and pre-war masonry houses with offices, hotels and retail premises next to flats. In a flat renovation the wires and circuits may be completely replaced, but the stairwell distribution board and the riser remain the building’s common property. The paper on a flat therefore describes the flat’s board and its circuits, not the whole building.

If you have rebuilt the flat’s electrical installation, order measurements while the board is still open and you know which circuits are new. If the work affected the stairwell board or the riser, talk to the managing company first. What you receive after such a renovation and how it works is explained on the page flat rewiring documents.

Three things will help in the request: who did the wiring work, whether there is a new diagram or only circuit labels in the board, and a photo of the open board.

Commercial premises for putting into use: access and the document route

In Riga the municipality’s City Development Department puts a building and parts of it into use. For construction cases opened after 31 December 2019, the application can be submitted in BIS (the Construction Information System). If the rebuilding of commercial premises has a building authority case, documents on the electrical work done and tests are added at acceptance; for what exactly is needed, see the page putting commercial premises into use.

Riga centre has a dense public transport network, but access by car is often limited. For the site visit that means one practical thing: agree in advance who will open the technical room and the main distribution board, and write whether it is possible to drive into the courtyard. If the premises receive visitors, state when the power may be switched off briefly for measurements.

Thermal camera inspection of a distribution board in a dense block

A thermal camera shows overheated connections in the distribution board and at protective devices when current flows through them. So for an office or shop this inspection is planned during working hours with the equipment switched on, not in empty premises. For a description of this work and the report, see the page thermal camera inspection of a distribution board.

Riga centre has dense development, parking restrictions and limited access to courtyards. If the building has several distribution boards in different wings or the cellar, write in your request how many there are and where. Then one visit can be planned in which the thermal camera inspection happens at the same time as the measurements.

Questions about these documents

What measuring instrument details must a test report contain?

The Annex 6 form of the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) has fields for the measuring instrument details and its calibration or verification certificate number. If you have been given a report without these entries, ask its author which instrument was used.

Do an office tenant and the building owner need separate reports?

Agree in the lease who orders and pays for the test. If the rented premises have their own distribution board, a report can be drawn up for that part. Testing the whole building is a different scope.

How do you request a site visit for premises with hard courtyard access?

In the form, write the address, the entrance, whether the courtyard gate is locked and who will open the board. After you send the form, we contact you within 5 minutes and agree a time when the premises and board are accessible.
