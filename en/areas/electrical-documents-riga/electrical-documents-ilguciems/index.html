In a production building or warehouse on industrial land it is a report for each distribution board, and if the premises have an explosive or chemically aggressive environment, the testing interval is far shorter than in a residential building.

Flat in a series-built block: measurements before accepting a renovation

The typical development of Iļģuciems is Soviet-era apartment estates, fragments of pre-war buildings, detached houses and industrial sites. If the wiring and distribution board of a flat in a series-built block are replaced during renovation, the time before you accept the work from the contractor and pay the last invoice is a good moment to check what has been done.

Before a renovation is accepted, the measurements in the flat cover the insulation resistance of each circuit, the phase-neutral loop impedance, the connection of the protective conductor to earthing and the tripping of the residual current devices (RCD). Then it is checked whether the circuits are labelled in the distribution board and whether the protective devices match the conductor cross-section. If something does not comply, the contractor still has a chance to put it right.

If the contractor is an electrician himself and gives you his own report, check that it contains the measuring instrument details and the results for each circuit. Also ask who issued his certificate, so that you can check it with the issuer or, if it is a construction practice certificate, in the BIS register. If any of this is missing, an independent measurement will show the real condition.

The report stays with you as evidence of the condition in which you received the installation. For how a flat is tested, see electrical installation testing in a flat.

Production building or warehouse: when testing is needed more often

Alongside housing, Iļģuciems has production, warehouses, retail and local services. Point 56 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027) ties the interval to the environment in the premises. For a production building this means that one building can have different intervals for different rooms.

Premises in a production building or warehouse How often tested Reports
With an explosive environment Once every two years Measurement report (Annex 6) and thermal camera report (Annex 7)
With a chemically aggressive environment Once a year The same reports
Without such an environment, for example an office or a dry warehouse Once every 10 years The same reports

Checked on 25 November 2026: Cabinet Regulation of 19 April 2016 No. 238 “Fire Safety Regulations”, sub-points 56.1 and 56.2. The regulations are in force until 1 January 2027; the text that will replace them had not yet been published on that date.

A company needs to know from its own activity and equipment which room counts as explosive or chemically aggressive. In your request, write which rooms these are, so that the right scope can be planned for each. For how company premises are tested, see electrical installation testing for businesses.

Apartment building: shared distribution boards and risers

Iļģuciems is a large residential neighbourhood on the left bank of the Daugava with an industrial history, and its apartment buildings have many stairwells. The shared part of a building is the incoming distribution board, the risers, the stairwell boards, the circuits of the basement and common areas, and the earthing. It is the joint property of the flat owners, so its testing has to be discussed with the building manager rather than ordered separately for each flat.

In a series-built block the earthing and risers may date from when the building was built, while the flats have been altered since. That is why the test also measures whether the protective conductors of the flats are continuously connected to the building’s earthing.

The building management agreement shows who exactly orders and pays for the test. Before the test it helps the manager to prepare a list of stairwells, access to the basement and a notice to residents about a short power cut. For testing an apartment building, see electrical installation testing in an apartment building.

Thermal camera for heavily loaded distribution boards

Iļģuciems has industrial sites where distribution boards carry high currents for many hours in a row. A loose connection in such a board heats up, and a thermal camera shows it before it becomes visible to the eye. The inspection is done during working hours while the equipment is running, because a connection does not heat up without current.

The fire safety regulations tie the thermal camera inspection to periodic testing, so in a production building with a two-year interval the thermal camera report is also drawn up every two years, together with the measurements.

In your request, write how many distribution boards there are and in which shifts the equipment runs at full load. For this work, see thermal camera inspection of distribution boards.

Questions about these documents

Where in production premises is testing needed more often than once every ten years?

Under the fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027), premises with an explosive environment must be tested once every two years, and premises with a chemically aggressive environment once a year. In other premises the interval is once every 10 years.

Who is responsible for testing the shared distribution boards in an apartment building?

The shared distribution boards and risers are the joint property of the flat owners, and the owners decide on their testing together with the building manager. The management agreement shows who exactly orders and pays for the work. Ask the manager when the last test took place.

Which measurements are taken in a flat before a renovation is accepted?

For each circuit the insulation resistance and the phase-neutral loop impedance are measured, and the connection of the protective conductor to earthing and the tripping of the residual current devices are checked. The results are entered in the report, and if a circuit does not comply, the contractor can put it right before the work is accepted.
