If you have a flat in a wooden or pre-war masonry building and its wiring has been replaced, you need a report on the new circuits. If the wiring has not been touched, a report on its condition is useful first. A shop or café on the ground floor has its own paperwork route.

A wooden house with mixed installations: a condition report

The typical buildings of Āgenskalns are wooden architecture, pre-war masonry houses, low-rise residential buildings, apartment buildings and public institutions. In a wooden house with several flats, one neighbour may have replaced the wiring a year ago, another twenty years ago, and a third not at all. The common incoming distribution board and the risers then feed very different installations.

If the wiring in your flat or house has not been replaced, the report describes what is in the distribution board and in the walls and what needs to be put right first; we tell you which measurements are needed after the site visit. It lets you decide on renovation instead of guessing. In a wooden house it is especially important to note the joints hidden under cladding or in the attic.

The report can be ordered by one owner for their own flat or by everyone together for the whole building. In the second case it also covers the common incoming distribution board and the risers, and the neighbours can share the cost.

If the report shows faults, an electrician repairs them, and a measurement report can be prepared after that. For how this work is done, see the page old electrical installation report.

After rewiring a flat: which circuits need an RCD

Āgenskalns is a central historic neighbourhood of Pārdaugava with a market, parks and a mixed urban setting, and many flats here are rebuilt in stages. If the installation is rebuilt, the new circuits have to meet the current construction standard. LBN 261-15, sub-point 28.1 (as amended by Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025), requires residual current protection with a tripping current of no more than 30 mA for these circuits:

  • general-use socket outlets up to 32 A used by persons without special electrical training;
  • socket outlets up to 32 A intended for connecting portable electrical equipment used outdoors;
  • electric lighting circuits in household electrical installations.

Checked 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 294 of 9 June 2015), sub-point 28.1, as amended by Cabinet Regulation No. 355 of 17 June 2025, likumi.lv.

If some circuits in a flat are new and some old, the protocol shows which have a residual current device and which do not. That helps you decide whether to rebuild the old circuits now or at the next stage of renovation.

A residual current device (RCD) switches off a circuit if current escapes along the wrong path, for example through a person. The measurements check whether it trips. For what you receive after such rebuilding, see the page flat rewiring documents.

A shop, café or office on the ground floor

People live in Āgenskalns, and retail, health care, educational institutions, catering and creative businesses work here. If a ground-floor flat is converted into a shop or office with a building authority case, the electrical measurements and diagram become part of the documents for putting it into use.

In Riga, the municipality’s City Development Department puts a building or part of it into use, and the municipality states a processing time of 10 working days, which can be extended to 4 months under the Administrative Procedure Law. So prepare the electrical papers before the application, not when the department asks for them.

If the premises will have medical or kitchen equipment, attach a list of it with power figures. It determines whether the existing permitted capacity is enough or whether an increase must be requested from Sadales tīkls before opening.

In your request, write the floor area, the equipment and whether there is a design. More: putting commercial premises into use.

A circuit-by-circuit protocol in a wooden house with several flats

Measurements are made circuit by circuit, so the results in the protocol are also listed by circuit: circuit number or name, protective device, measured insulation resistance and loop impedance, and RCD tripping where there is one. In a wooden house with several flats, the protocol has to show clearly which circuits belong to the flat and which to the building’s common part.

Before the test, agree with the neighbours that the electricity in the building may be switched off for a short while during measurements, and with the building manager or house representative who will open the common distribution board. If the circuits are not labelled on the board door, try to note beforehand which switch cuts off which room.

What each line of the protocol means is explained on the page what a test protocol contains.

Questions about these documents

Which circuits in a residential building need a residual current device?

Under LBN 261-15, sub-point 28.1, a device of no more than 30 mA protects general-use socket outlets up to 32 A, outlets up to 32 A for portable equipment used outdoors, and household lighting circuits. There is also a separate requirement for the incoming supply of a detached house.

Does every electrical circuit have to be listed in the protocol?

Measurements are made on each circuit, and in the protocol each circuit has its own entry with the protective device and the results. If the protocol has only one overall figure for the whole distribution board, it cannot show which circuit complies and which does not.

How do you prepare for a test in a wooden house with several flats?

Arrange access to the common distribution board and the flats, warn the neighbours about a short power cut and note which switches feed which rooms. In your request, state the number of flats and whether the test is needed for one flat or for the whole building.
