The short answer: one diagram for the whole property, measurements for each building and earthing that reaches every metal structure. If the land has no electricity yet, you start with a connection, while panels and a charger have their own papers for Sadales tīkls.

Stable, barn and garage: earthing and a diagram for the whole property

The typical development of Kleisti is detached houses, farm buildings, equestrian and sports grounds, forests and undeveloped land. On a property with several buildings, electricity from the house distribution board may run to a stable, barn or garage through a buried cable. Each building may have its own board, and then the diagram has to show where each is fed from and what protection is at the start of each line.

A stable has damp, dust and animals, so earthing and the protective bonding to metal structures, such as stalls and watering equipment, are particularly important in testing. For what outbuilding papers include and how they are prepared, see electrical documents for a sauna, garage and outbuilding.

Kleisti has forests, damp lowlands and long distances between structures. On a long line to a distant building the phase-neutral loop impedance is higher than on a short one, and it is this measurement that shows whether the circuit breaker at the start of the line will disconnect a fault at its end too. If the result is borderline, the installation report recommends different protection or a cable with a larger cross-section.

In your request, write:

  • how many buildings have electricity and whether each has its own distribution board;
  • the approximate distances between buildings and whether the cables are buried;
  • whether there is an old diagram or design;
  • which buildings house animals or have damp rooms.

Electricity for land without buildings: the application and the customer’s part

Kleisti is a spacious and sparsely built neighbourhood, reached by Kleistu iela and the local road network. If your plot has no buildings yet, an electricity connection is applied for at e-st.lv. Sadales tīkls builds its part up to the distribution board, and everything after it is your installation, which an electrician prepares. The fee depends on the capacity you want and the distance to the existing low-voltage line.

If you plan to build on the land, consider whether you first need a temporary construction site connection: for it Sadales tīkls requires a valid building permit. For how a connection to a plot works, see electricity connection for a plot. In the form, attach the location of the plot on a map and the cadastre number if there is no address yet.

If the plot is far from the existing line, Sadales tīkls first assesses whether the network has to be extended, and then the time and fee are different. In your request, also state where on the plot you want the distribution board, because that determines how long your cable to further buildings will be.

EV charger on a rural property

Public transport and access are less dense in Kleisti, so an electric car charger at home can be a practical decision. In its advice on charging at a private house, Sadales tīkls writes that no application is needed if the existing connection capacity is enough, and mentions a three-phase 20–25 A connection, that is, 13.8–17.3 kW. A charger with dynamic load management may make it possible to do without a capacity increase.

The check compares the charger power with the incoming supply and the other load, especially if stable or workshop equipment runs at the same time, and assesses the cable and protection up to the charger location. The charger is fitted by an installer. For what the work includes, see electric car charger connection check.

Panels on a barn roof: how large a microgenerator may be

The neighbourhood serves housing, sport, recreation, business activity and land management, and a large barn or stable roof may tempt you to fit a large solar system. Sadales tīkls sets a limit: a microgenerator is up to 16 A on each phase, that is, up to 3.7 kW on a single-phase and up to 11.1 kW on a three-phase connection. A larger system is a power station with a different, more complex connection procedure.

Checked on 25 November 2026: Sadales tīkls, “Connecting a microgenerator”; a microgenerator is up to 16 A on each phase (3.7 kW single-phase, 11.1 kW three-phase).

So before ordering panels, find out how many phases your connection has and in which building the inverter will be. The installer needs a construction specialist certificate for generating equipment, because Sadales tīkls requires it for installation and inverter setup. After that we check the system and draw up the settings protocol. For the whole route, see connecting a microgenerator.

If the inverter is in the barn but the meter is by the house, there must be a line between them with its own protection, and it is shown in the diagram. The Sadales tīkls requirements include a circuit breaker of up to 16 A between the inverter and the meter, so this point is checked before the protocol is drawn up.

Price for a diagram, connection and charger papers

For an as-built diagram of a property with several buildings the price is set after the site visit: it depends on the number of distribution boards and circuits and how many cable routes have to be traced on site. For work and documents for Sadales tīkls the price is set after the site visit, because it matters whether the incoming supply and distribution board suit the capacity needed. For a charger connection check the price depends on the distance to the distribution board and the existing connection capacity. The site visit is paid: €40.

Prices cover the electrician’s work and the documents. Sadales tīkls and building authority fees are separate.

Questions about these documents

How large a microgenerator can be connected to a single-phase network?

Up to 3.7 kW, because Sadales tīkls defines a microgenerator as up to 16 A on each phase. On a three-phase connection the limit is 11.1 kW. If you want a larger system on a single-phase connection, you first have to switch to three phases.

How do you connect electricity to land without buildings?

The application is submitted at e-st.lv, and Sadales tīkls builds its part up to the distribution board. Beyond that, an electrician of your choice prepares the installation, and when it is ready, you submit the confirmation. A temporary construction site connection needs a valid building permit.

Does the earthing of a stable need testing?

If a stable has electricity, the earthing and the protective bonding to metal structures are measured in the same way as in other buildings. This is part of the periodic testing provided for by the fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027). It is particularly important in a damp and dusty environment, so write in your request that the building houses animals.
