After rebuilding: insulation measurement before putting into use

The typical buildings of Grīziņkalns are pre-war masonry and wooden tenements, courtyard buildings, renovated blocks and small commercial premises. If a courtyard building is converted into flats or a workshop, or a house has its electrical installation renewed with a building authority case, it has to be shown before putting into use that the new installation is safe. The first indicator is insulation resistance.

Point 54 of the construction standard LBN 261-15 on internal electrical installations of buildings provides that the insulation resistance of electrical installations is tested before acceptance for use, and the minimum values are given in Table 2 of Annex 6. Circuits with a voltage up to 500 V are measured with a 500 V test voltage, and the resistance must be greater than 1.0 MΩ.

Checked 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation No. 294 of 9 June 2015), point 54 and Annex 6, Table 2, likumi.lv.

  2. Site visit The electrician compares the board and circuits with the design or work description and agrees when the power can be switched off.
  3. Insulation measurement Insulation resistance is measured on each circuit while equipment is disconnected.
  4. Loop and earthing Phase-neutral loop resistance and earthing connections are measured.
  5. Documents The results are drawn up in a report and protocol that are added to the other documents for putting into use.

If a measurement on a circuit does not reach the standard, the report records it, and an electrician puts the circuit right before putting into use. A repeat measurement shows that the problem has been fixed, and only then is the document added to the building authority case.

If the wiring has not been replaced: a report on the old part

Grīziņkalns is a historic densely built neighbourhood on the eastern side of the centre. One building may have flats where the wiring was replaced recently and flats where it has not been touched for decades. If the wiring in your flat or house has not been replaced, a report is useful before deciding on renovation: it describes what is in the distribution board and walls and what needs putting right; measurements are agreed after the site visit.

A report does not replace renovation. If it mentions faults, an electrician repairs them, and then a measurement report on the new condition can be prepared. For how the report is prepared and what is written in it, see the page old electrical installation report.

For the report it helps to know when the building was last renovated, whether the board has circuit breakers or old fuses and whether neighbours recently replaced the riser. Write this in your request, and the unclear places can be addressed straight away at the site visit.

A flat above a café: a separate report for each

People live in Grīziņkalns, and creative businesses, shops, cafés and service outlets work in the same buildings. Flat rebuilding documents relate to the flat’s distribution board and its circuits. A ground-floor café has its own board, its own equipment and a different load, so it needs its own report.

If the café kitchen has powerful equipment, it is useful to know its total power and the size of the existing main protective device. That shows whether the board and circuits suit the load and whether a capacity question for Sadales tīkls will have to be added to the papers.

If you are rewiring a flat, order measurements while the board is open and you still remember which circuits are new. After the work, keep the report and diagram with the flat’s other papers. For what you receive in this case, see the page flat rewiring documents.

An as-built diagram for a house with a courtyard building

Grīziņkalns has dense courtyards, limited parking and historic building access. If a courtyard building is connected through the street house distribution board, the cable between the two buildings may run through the cellar, a wall or the ground. An as-built diagram, or electrical installation diagram, shows how the installation was actually built: from the incoming board to each circuit and between the buildings.

For the diagram to be drawn, the electrician must reach all distribution boards. In your request, write how many buildings are in the courtyard, where the boards are and who can unlock the cellar. If there is an old diagram or design, attach it: then only the differences have to be checked on site. This work is described on the page as-built diagram.

Questions about these documents

Are insulation measurements needed after rewiring a flat?

Point 54 of LBN 261-15 requires insulation resistance to be tested before electrical installations are accepted for use. If the rewiring was part of rebuilding with a building authority case, the measurement is needed. If there was no case, the measurement shows that the new circuits are in order, and the report stays with the flat’s papers.

What distinguishes a measurement report from an electrical installation report?

A measurement report is a record of results: each circuit, the measured value and whether it meets the standard. An electrical installation report is a wider written assessment: it has a description of the site visit and a conclusion on whether the installation can be used and what has to be done. If you do not know which you need, write who is asking for the paper and why.

Will a flat report do for a ground-floor café?

No. A flat report describes the flat’s distribution board and circuits. A café has its own board and equipment, so it needs a report on its own premises. It can be prepared on the same visit if both boards are accessible.
