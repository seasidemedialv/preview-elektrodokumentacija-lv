For the building administrator it is testing of the shared part: incoming distribution board, rising mains and stairwell boards. For a flat owner it is their flat board and circuits, for example after renovation or before applying for more capacity. The two need different reports, each at a different time.

Stairwell boards and rising mains: testing the building

The typical development of Zolitūde is Soviet-era apartment estates, newer developments, retail and public buildings. A Soviet-era high-rise has one or more incoming distribution boards, rising mains through all floors and a board on every floor of each stairwell. This is the shared part of the building, and it is tested in the building test, not in the flat test.

The test also measures earthing and whether the protective conductor from the incoming board reaches every floor without a break. If the building has lightning protection on the roof, that also goes in the building report, not in flat reports.

Before testing it helps the administrator to prepare the number of stairwells and floors, the previous report, if there is one, and access to the basement and incoming room. Warn residents of a short power cut in the stairwell. Newer developments also have the documents handed over by the developer showing the original state.

For how apartment building testing goes, see the page apartment building electrical installation testing.

A flat after rewiring: what you receive

In a Soviet-era flat, rewiring may mean a new board, a different division into circuits and wires on new routes. In a newer development it may mean kitchen alterations or underfloor heating. In both cases a measurement report on the new circuits and an up-to-date diagram showing which circuit feeds which room are useful after the work.

If the flat is in a newer development, ask the administrator for the original flat diagram before rewiring. With it, it is easier to show what has been changed and what has stayed as it was, and the new diagram does not have to be drawn from scratch.

The report and diagram stay with the flat owner. A copy can also be given to the building manager if they ask for it together with the approval.

Order measurements while the renovator is still available and can fix what does not comply. If the work affected a rising main or the wire to the stairwell board, this has to be agreed with the building manager. For what you receive after such rewiring, see the page flat rewiring documents.

More capacity for a flat: approval from the building manager

If you plan an induction hob, electric water heater or heat pump in a high-rise flat, you may need more permitted capacity. Sadales tīkls states that a flat owner must agree any changes to the building’s internal electrical networks with the building manager in writing and attach the approval document to the service application. Before applying on e-st.lv, prepare:

  • the building manager’s written approval;
  • the owner’s approval, if another person, such as a tenant, submits the application;
  • the required capacity and number of phases, which can be calculated from a list of appliances;
  • information on whether the flat board and the wire to the stairwell suit more capacity.

Checked on 25 November 2026: Sadales tīkls “Load changes” (sadalestikls.lv).

The building manager may want to know whether the rising main will take a larger load. At the site visit the electrician looks at the flat board and feed and prepares the papers for the application. For the steps, see the page increasing the permitted capacity.

If it turns out that the rising main is not suitable for more capacity, the question moves to the whole building and is dealt with by the flat owners together with the building manager. Then the flat application should wait until there is a solution for the building.

If a bank asks for a report

People live in Zolitūde, and there is retail, education and everyday services here. If you are buying a flat here with a mortgage, the question may arise whether the bank needs any electrical paper. Whether such a paper is needed is decided by the bank itself. It or its valuer may ask for a document on the condition of the installation, especially if the flat is bought with a plan to alter it.

If a bank asks for an installation report, ask it to put in writing what it must contain. The report holds a description of the site visit and a conclusion; it can also be useful to the buyer, to know which work will have to be done. More: electrical installation report for a bank.

If the seller orders the report before the deal, the buyer and bank see the condition during negotiations. If the buyer orders it, agree with the seller on access to the flat and board and on the power being switched off briefly.

Questions about these documents

What consent is attached if the permitted capacity of a flat is increased?

The building manager’s written approval. Sadales tīkls states that a flat owner must agree changes to the building’s internal electrical networks with the building manager and attach the approval to the application. If a tenant submits the application, the owner’s approval is needed too.

Does a bank need an electrical installation report for a mortgage?

That is determined by the particular lender. If a bank or valuer asks for a document on the condition of the installation, ask them to write down what it must contain and order exactly that. A report can also be useful to the buyer themselves.

How can a building manager request testing of a stairwell distribution board?

In the form, write the building address, number of stairwells and floors, whether there is a previous report and who will provide access to the incoming room and basement. After you send the form, we contact you within 5 minutes and agree on the site visit.
