Old report or new: what the fire safety regulations set

Point 525 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027) says the following. A test report on electrical installation insulation resistance, phase-neutral loop impedance, earthing installation resistance, the earthing conductor continuity bond and the lightning protection system drawn up after 19 April 2016 is valid until the next such test. So before ordering a new test, first look at what you already have:

  • the report date: whether it is after 19 April 2016;
  • whether the report has all the parts: insulation, loop, earthing, earthing conductor continuity and lightning protection, if there is any;
  • whether anything in the installation has been changed since the report date: new circuits, a different board, an extension;
  • whether the report gives measuring instrument details and the calibration or verification certificate number.

Checked on 25 November 2026: Cabinet Regulation of 19 April 2016 No. 238 “Fire Safety Regulations”, point 525. The regulations cease to be in force on 2 January 2027; as at 25 November 2026 no text had been published saying how old reports will be assessed after that.

If something is missing from the report or the installation has been changed, the report no longer describes the present condition, and a new test is worth doing.

If the report is lost but you know who drew it up, ask them for a copy. If there is no copy, a new report is the only way to show measurement results to a buyer, tenant or administrator.

A wooden or brick detached house by the railway: what has to be tested

The typical development of Zasulauks is pre-war wooden and brick buildings, detached houses, apartment buildings, railway and commercial areas. In a detached house the test covers everything: the incoming supply, main distribution board, every circuit, earthing and lightning protection, if there is any. If the house has a shed, garage or sauna with a separate cable, write that, because they are part of the installation too.

Being near the railway or a main road changes nothing for a house’s electrical papers. What matters more is how many buildings are on the plot and whether one incoming supply feeds them all, because this determines how many boards and cables have to be measured.

In a pre-war house the old wiring may survive next to the new. If the wiring in your house has not been replaced or you do not know when it was done, write that in your request. Then it is known in advance that, in addition to a test report, an installation report with recommendations on what to put right can be useful.

If you will put the house into use after alterations, the measurements become part of the building authority documents. For what private house testing includes, see the page electrical installation testing in a private house.

If the policy issuer asks for papers

The insurer decides which electrical document a house or shop owner has to submit. If it asks you for a test report or an installation report, ask it to write down exactly what is needed and how old a document is acceptable. Then you do not have to order more than is asked.

An installation report differs from a test report in that it also contains a conclusion on the condition of the installation and recommendations. If the document is needed after damage, for example after water damage or a short circuit, the installation report describes what can be seen after the event. More: electrical installation report for insurance.

After damage, first find out whether the insurer wants its own expert to inspect before any work. Only after that let an electrician change anything in the board, otherwise it can no longer be shown what the damage looked like.

Shop, office or workshop near the station

People live in Zasulauks, and there is transport, retail, offices and local services here. A building with a shop on the ground floor and flats above may have one shared incoming supply and several distribution boards. A report can be drawn up for each user’s board, and who orders testing of the shared incoming supply is agreed with the building owner.

A workshop with metalworking or woodworking machines has three-phase circuits, and measurements on them are done for each phase. If a machine has its own board or a separate cable from the incoming supply, mention that too.

In your request, write how many boards there are, who is the building owner and who the tenant, and when power in the premises can be switched off briefly. For how testing of business premises goes, see the page electrical installation testing for a business.

Questions about these documents

How long is a previously drawn up electrical installation report valid?

Under point 525 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (MK 238, in force until 1 January 2027), a test report drawn up after 19 April 2016 is valid until the next test. If the installation has been changed since, the report no longer describes what is actually there.

Will a report made several years ago do for insurance?

The insurer decides that. Ask in writing how old a document it accepts and whether it must be a test report or an installation report. If anything in the house has changed since the report, a new document will be more accurate.

What should the form say if a house has both a flat and a shop?

Write how many distribution boards there are and which premises each feeds, whether the document is needed for the flat, the shop or both, and who owns the building. Attach photos of the boards and the previous report, if there is one.
