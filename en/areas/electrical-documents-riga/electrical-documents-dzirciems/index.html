For a flat in an apartment building it answers whether the wiring can continue to be used. School, clinic or shop premises need a report or test report together with periodic testing, and there the deadlines in the regulations matter too.

A flat in an apartment building: a report for the owner and tenant

The typical buildings of Dzirciems are apartment buildings, educational and medical facilities, pockets of private houses and commercial buildings. A flat may need a report at two moments: when the owner wants to know whether the old wiring can stay, and when the flat is let or sold. In both cases the report gives a clear description, not a general impression.

The report contains a description of the flat’s distribution board, the protective devices and a conclusion with recommendations; we tell you which measurements the flat needs after the site visit. If you only need measurement results, a test report will do; if you need an assessment and recommendations, order an electrical installation report. An electrical installation report costs from €180.

The report’s recommendations help you decide what has to be done before a tenant moves in and what can be put off until the next renovation. An electrician repairs faults, and after that you can order a measurement report on the new condition.

If a tenant lives in the flat, agree a time with them, because the power will be switched off briefly during measurements, and the fridge and computers are better switched off beforehand. For how a flat test goes, see the page electrical installation testing in a flat.

A school, clinic or shop: a report for the owner of the premises

People live in Dzirciems, and there is health care, education, retail and services here. Institution premises have many circuits, several distribution boards and equipment that must not be switched off at just any moment, such as medical apparatus or servers. So the test is planned with the institution’s facilities manager and split into parts.

If the institution’s building is managed by the municipality or another owner but a tenant uses the premises, find out who orders the test. Arrange access to all distribution boards, including those in another wing or the cellar.

In your request, write the floor area, the number of boards, whether the building has lightning protection and whether a thermal camera inspection is needed with the measurements. If there is a previous report, attach it: it shows the circuit numbering and the date from which the next test deadline is counted.

For how business and institution premises are tested, see the page electrical installation testing for businesses.

How often to test: intervals under the current regulations

Dzirciems is a residential and institutional neighbourhood of Pārdaugava between Iļģuciems, Āgenskalns and Ķīpsala, so one street here may have a residential building, a school and a medical institution. Point 56 of the fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) sets the testing frequency by the environment in the property, not by the type of building:

Environment in the property or territory Electrical installation testing What it includes
Ordinary environment Once every 10 years Electrical installation, including earthing and lightning protection
Explosive environment Once every two years The same scope
Chemically aggressive environment Once a year The same scope

Checked 25 November 2026: Cabinet Regulation No. 238 of 19 April 2016, “Fire Safety Regulations”, point 56. The regulations are in force until 1 January 2027; the text of their successor has not yet been published on this date, so the 10-year interval is not permanent.

For a school or clinic the ordinary 10-year interval applies if the premises have no explosive or chemically aggressive environment. If a laboratory has one, the interval is shorter. More: periodic electrical installation testing.

The interval is counted from the date of the previous report. If there is no report or it is unknown when the last test was done, it is safer to test now and start counting afresh from it.

Residual current devices on many circuits

Medical and educational properties and newer flats have many residual current devices (RCD), which switch off a circuit if current escapes along the wrong path. Testing them means measuring at what current and how fast each device trips. The test button on a device only shows that the mechanism moves.

For each device the report records the rated residual current, the measured tripping current and time. If a device does not trip or trips too slowly, an electrician replaces it, and then the test is repeated for that device.

An RCD test can be ordered separately or with the measurements. If the premises have many devices, write their number in your request. For this work, see the page residual current device testing.

Questions about these documents

How often must the electrical installation in educational or medical premises be tested?

The fire safety regulations currently in force (Cabinet Regulation No. 238, in force until 1 January 2027) set testing once every 10 years, once every two years in an explosive environment and once a year in a chemically aggressive environment. They have no separate interval for schools or clinics. An institution’s internal requirements may be stricter.

Is a residual current device tested separately?

It can be done either separately or together with the other measurements. The Annex 6 report of the fire safety regulations does not mention the RCD as a separate part, so if you specifically need the device test results, say so in your request.

Who orders a test in a flat with a tenant?

Agree in the rental contract who orders it. Arrange the time with the tenant, because the power is switched off briefly during measurements.
