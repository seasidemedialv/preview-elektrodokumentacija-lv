As-built documentation for a new waterfront project

The typical development of Klīversala is historic masonry and wooden buildings, offices, apartment projects and waterfront development areas. In a new project the electrical installation is created in several stages and by several subcontractors, and by handover part of it is inside walls, ceilings and floors. So the documents have to be collected as the work goes on, not at the end.

Point 53 of the building standard for internal electrical installations, LBN 261-15, sets which documents are attached to the acceptance statement (if the relevant work has been carried out). The table shows those most relevant to the electrician’s work:

Document (LBN 261-15) When it is created
Set of drawings corrected under designer’s supervision (53.1) During the work, when solutions change; at the end it matches what has been built
Equipment data sheets and material conformity certificates (53.2) When equipment and cables are delivered and installed
Acceptance records of concealed works on electrical installations (53.3) Before the wires are covered with finishes or concrete
Reports on testing conductor resistance, the phase-neutral loop and earthing (53.4) When the installation is finished and can be measured

Checked on 25 November 2026: LBN 261-15 (Cabinet Regulation of 9 June 2015 No. 294), point 53, likumi.lv. A concealed works record is not recreated after the work has been covered up; ask the building authority what it accepts instead.

If the construction manager or client wants the measurements and diagram ready by the time of the submission to the building authority, arrange the electrician’s visits by stage now. More: as-built documentation.

If the project has underground parking or technical rooms in the basement, there are more distribution boards and cable routes than in an ordinary building. The as-built diagram has to show all of them with location and labelling, so that the building manager later knows which board feeds which floor and where to look for a cable.

Office handover: what to receive from the builder

People live in Klīversala, and it has offices, hospitality, culture and active property development. If an office is fitted out for a tenant or a new owner takes over premises from a developer, the electrical installation papers are as important as the keys. Without them it is hard to prove later what was built and how, and every change begins with investigating the installation.

Before you sign the acceptance, ask the builder for an as-built diagram of the premises’ distribution boards, measurement reports and concealed works records for the premises you are taking over. In Riga, in cases opened after 31 December 2019, the application for putting into use is submitted in BIS, so agree who uploads the documents and when they will be available.

A tenant also benefits from knowing what capacity is allocated to the premises and which distribution board is their responsibility. This is written into the lease or its annex, and then there are no disputes over who orders the next test.

If some papers are missing, measurements and a diagram can be prepared anew after a site visit. It does not replace concealed works records. For how commercial premises are handed over, see putting commercial premises into use.

Flat in a building already in use: when new measurements are needed

Klīversala is a central area on the left bank of the Daugava between Āgenskalns and the river embankment, with both historic buildings and new projects. If the building has already been put into use, the flat’s electrical installation was part of that handover, and copies of the documents may be held by the developer, the manager or in the building authority case in BIS. Ask for them if you have not been given them.

New measurements are needed when the installation itself changes: you add circuits, move walls, fit underfloor heating or replace the distribution board. After such work a report and an updated diagram show what is actually there, and they are useful if you sell or let the flat. If the work affects the riser or stairwell board, arrange it with the manager first.

If the flat is in a historic building rather than a new project, the starting point is different. Then, before planning changes, a site visit and an installation report on the existing installation are useful, because there may be no handover documents at all.

For what you receive after alterations and how it works, see flat electrical installation alteration documents.

Questions about these documents

What is a concealed works record and who draws it up?

It is a record of work that is covered up afterwards, for example wires in walls or in the floor. Sub-point 53.3 of LBN 261-15 requires such records to be attached to the acceptance statement. They are drawn up during the work while it is visible; ask the construction manager or the building authority which parties sign in your case.

Does a new flat need measurements if the building is already in use?

If the installation has not been changed, the flat was part of the building’s handover, so first ask for copies of those documents. New measurements are useful after changes, such as new circuits or a different distribution board, and also if you want your own report before selling or letting.

Which documents should you ask the builder for before you hand over an office?

An as-built diagram of the premises’ distribution boards and circuits, measurement reports, concealed works records for the electrical installations and equipment data sheets. Also agree who adds these documents to the handover case in BIS and when, so that they do not appear only after the contract has been signed.
